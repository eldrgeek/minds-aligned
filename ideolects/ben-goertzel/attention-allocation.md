Attention allocation is Goertzel's name for the processes that distribute limited cognitive resources across atoms, goals, and candidate computations so that a real-world general intelligence can act under hard resource constraint. In CogPrime/OpenCog, attention values (and related dynamics) mark which knowledge and procedures are currently important enough to receive inference steps, learning effort, or action-selection focus, making attention a first-class control layer over the Atomspace rather than a peripheral UI metaphor.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel treats attention allocation as the critical factor that shapes real-world general intelligence precisely because unbounded reasoning and learning are infeasible. The distinctive move is to couple nonlinear-dynamical attention (including economic and information-geometric formulations) tightly to logic, learning, and action so that combinatorial explosion in inference is controlled by synergy with attention, not only by smarter search heuristics. That rules out AGI designs that assume unlimited compute or treat “attention” only as a neural soft-weighting mechanism, and it departs from everyday cognitive-psychology talk of attention by making resource economics over a shared knowledge graph an architectural primitive of AGI.
