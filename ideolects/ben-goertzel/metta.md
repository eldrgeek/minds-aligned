MeTTa (Meta Type Talk) is Goertzel’s programming language for OpenCog Hyperon: a minimal meta-language for symbols, groundings, variables, types, substitutions, and pattern matching, with primitives for defining new type systems and domain-specific languages. Informally it is Hyperon’s lowest-level “language of thought”—the substrate in which algorithms for learning more particular knowledge representations can run, and in which those algorithms themselves may be represented—formally approachable as systems of reflective metagraph rewrite rules.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel does not present MeTTa as a conventional application language or as another Lisp dialect for scripting agents; it is the *cognitive meta-layer* for integrative AGI, meant so that learning, inference, and self-modification share one rewriteable metagraph medium. That rules out bolting AGI processes together only through external APIs and opaque neural weights with no common symbolic-procedural language, and it departs from standard “knowledge representation languages” by treating reflection and self-rewriting as design requirements: the language must host both particular thoughts and the processes that invent new representations of thought.
