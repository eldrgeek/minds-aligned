Inference control is Goertzel's term for the hardest practical problem in logical reasoning for AGI: not the rule-set and not uncertainty management alone, but the ongoing choice of *which* inference steps to take, in what order, and in which contexts, so that declarative learning remains scalable. In the CogPrime/OpenCog line, probabilistic logic without effective control is treated as unscalable; control is the mechanism that turns a large, uncertain knowledge graph into a usable engine for growing declarative knowledge under limited computation and attention.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel insists that inference control cannot be solved by looking at logic alone. Effective control must arise from the intersection of logical inference with other cognitive processes—especially attention allocation, procedure learning, and the wider dynamics of an integrative architecture—rather than from a pure proof-search heuristic sealed inside a theorem prover. That rules out both exhaustive classical inference and “fire all rules” production systems as AGI strategies, and it departs from standard automated-reasoning usage of “control” by making cross-process synergy the design target: which step is worth taking is a whole-mind decision, not a local logic-engine scheduling trick.
