Patternist philosophy (or patternism) is Goertzel's working ontology of mind: intelligence and mentality are understood primarily in terms of patterns—recurring, compressible regularities in data, behavior, and structure—and a mind is a system that recognizes, creates, transforms, and organizes such patterns in service of goals within an environment. In this usage, "pattern" is the basic unit of cognitive analysis, bridging concrete human functions and abstract AGI theory without reducing mind to either pure computation-as-syntax or pure behaviorism.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel deploys patternism as the philosophical backbone of his AGI program, not as decorative metaphysics. It grounds design choices from Atomspace representation through cognitive synergy and Hyperon rewrite dynamics: what matters is whether processes increase the system's ability to find and use patterns that matter for goals. That rules out AGI roadmaps that treat intelligence as a single loss-function fit or as disembodied theorem proving without pattern economy, and it departs from mainstream philosophy-of-mind labels (functionalism, connectionism, classical computationalism) by making *pattern intensity, emergence, and organization* the shared language for both human cognitive concreteness and machine architecture—explicitly tying everyday intelligent functions to the engineering of general intelligence.
