MOSES (Meta-Optimizing Semantic Evolutionary Search) is Goertzel’s program-evolution engine for integrative AGI: a procedure-learning system meant, at Marr’s computational level, to play a role comparable to evolutionary mechanisms in human thought—evolving candidate programs quickly, accurately, and reliably enough to solve hard problems rather than only toy benchmarks. It treats learned skills and behaviors as evolving program populations whose structure and semantics are optimized together, supplying the procedural arm of architectures that also host probabilistic logic and attention dynamics.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel does not use “evolutionary computation” as a general metaphor for gradual improvement; MOSES is a specific competence claim about program learning under AGI requirements—hard problems, reliability, and integration with other cognitive processes. That rules out treating procedure learning as mere policy gradients or as isolated genetic programming demos, and it departs from standard evolutionary algorithms by emphasizing meta-optimization and semantic representation of programs so that evolved procedures can participate in cognitive synergy with declarative inference and attention, not sit in a black-box fitness loop outside the rest of the mind.
