The Embodied Communication Prior (ECP) is Goertzel's way of defining real-world general intelligence without pure mathematical over-generality or pure anthropomorphism: general intelligence is characterized by reference to simplicity of goals and environments, where simplicity is gauged by how easily an entity can be communicated within a community of embodied agents. Augmented by assumptions about the statistical structure of communicated knowledge, this yields a picture of intelligence as distinct but interacting memory and cognitive subsystems for procedural, declarative, sensory/episodic, attentional, and intentional knowledge.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel uses the ECP as a *prior* on what kinds of minds and worlds matter for AGI—those in which intelligence is measured by effective communication and action among bodies sharing an environment—not as a linguistics slogan or a soft claim that “embodiment helps.” It rules out definitions of intelligence as unrestricted program search over arbitrary computable rewards, and also rules out equating AGI with human imitation alone. The distinctive payoff is architectural: the ECP is the conceptual parent of cognitive synergy theory, arguing that the multi-memory decomposition of mind is forced by the communication-and-embodiment prior rather than chosen for engineering convenience.
