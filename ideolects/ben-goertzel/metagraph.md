A metagraph, in Goertzel's technical usage, is a typed hypergraph in which types are assigned to hyperedges and their targets, and in which an edge's targets may connect not only to ordinary nodes but to whole links. Directed typed metagraphs partition each edge's targets into input, output, and lateral sets so that “metapaths” can chain edges; a menagerie of morphisms (catamorphisms, anamorphisms, chronomorphisms, and related folds and unfolds) then provides a general algebra for deterministic and stochastic processes over this structure.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Metagraphs are not a generic synonym for knowledge graphs. Goertzel deploys them as the mathematical and computational substrate for Hyperon-era AGI knowledge representation and for MeTTa-style reflective rewriting—the place where pattern matching, dependent/probabilistic types, and multi-paradigm cognitive algorithms can share one graph-theoretic home. This rules out flat triple stores and ordinary directed graphs as sufficient for integrative AGI memory, and it departs from standard hypergraph mining by elevating edge-to-edge targeting, typed metapaths, and fold/unfold morphisms into the core language of thought rather than optional graph sugar.
