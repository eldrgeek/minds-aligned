Pattern mining, in Goertzel's AGI work, is the algorithmic discovery of recurrent, interesting structure—especially frequent and interesting subhypergraphs—in a knowledge store so that a system can form higher-level patterns usable for further reasoning and learning. Implementations such as FISHGRAM treat the Atomspace-style hypergraph as the mining domain, surfacing candidate patterns that can feed concept formation, inference, and integration with perception rather than only producing statistical association reports for a human analyst.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel frames pattern mining as part of a patternist account of intelligence: minds grow by recognizing and compressing patterns in the world and in their own knowledge. Mining is not an offline data-mining side tool; it is a cognitive process meant to interoperate with probabilistic logic, deep-perception pipelines, and other OpenCog processes so that discovered patterns become live knowledge. That rules out treating AGI learning as only gradient fitting of black-box models or only axiom-driven deduction, and it departs from commercial graph-mining usage by targeting general intelligence—interestingness and reuse inside a self-modifying mind—rather than dashboard analytics alone.
