Dual network names Goertzel's claim that a mature mind's global organization is a single attractor that simultaneously supports a hierarchical (categorical, compositional, top-down) network and an associative (spreading, context-sensitive, lateral) network over the same pattern-processes. Hierarchy supplies abstraction and control; association supplies flexible retrieval and analogy; neither layer is a secondary add-on to the other.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel treats the dual network as a structural necessity of general intelligence, not as optional hybrid wiring. Pure taxonomies without rich association, and pure associative nets without hierarchical control, are ruled out as insufficient attractors for mind. The idea later informs how knowledge is laid out in integrative AGI systems—graph memories that must support both inheritance-style structure and Hebbian-style linkage—rather than the everyday AI sense of “two networks fused at the end.” The distinctive move is that hierarchy and association are *superposed dynamics of one system*, culminating the Psynet attractor hierarchy, not two modules communicating through an API.
