GOLEM (goal-oriented learning meta-architecture) is Goertzel's high-level AGI meta-architecture designed so a system can radically improve its general intelligence while preserving its initial goals. As a meta-architecture, GOLEM wraps around a variety of base-level AGI systems and uses a powerful narrow-AI subcomponent as a probability estimator, structuring self-modification so goal preservation and intelligence amplification are jointly feasible rather than traded off.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> GOLEM does architectural work on the beneficial-AGI problem: it is Goertzel's concrete response to the worry that open-ended self-improvement inevitably destroys original goals. The design targets systems that are massively and self-improvingly intelligent, probably beneficial, and almost surely not destructive—ruling out both frozen non-self-modifying agents and unconstrained recursive self-improvement without goal-stability machinery. It departs from pure utility-maximizer orthodoxy in the alignment literature by treating goal preservation as an engineered meta-architectural property of an integrative AGI stack, not solely as a formal decision-theoretic constraint on a single reward function.
