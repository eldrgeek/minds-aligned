A mindplex is Goertzel's term for a composite mind assembled from individual constituents—humans, AIs, or both—together with an encompassing collective layer, such that both the parts and the whole concurrently possess cohesive “theatres of consciousness.” The concept sits between single-agent AGI and a pure global brain: a mindplex has structure (local minds plus a collective mind) rather than only anonymous network effects, and it can be engineered as well as observed.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel (with Montes) treats mindplexes as a practical architecture for AGI-era society, not merely a poetic description of groups. The term does work by licensing designs in which individual agency and collective intelligence co-exist as first-class cognitive layers—each with its own global workspace-like dynamics—while ethics and value alignment are built into the composite's functional architecture (including proposals such as AGI on distributed ledger technology). This rules out both pure solipsistic agent models and flat swarm models that erase individual theatres of mind, and it departs from standard multi-agent systems talk by insisting on concurrent, multi-scale consciousness rather than only message-passing coordination.
