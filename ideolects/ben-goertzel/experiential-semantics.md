Experiential semantics is Goertzel's (and collaborators') foundation for Probabilistic Logic Networks: the truth and meaning of propositions are interpreted relative to the history of observations and actions of an intelligent agent embodied in a world, rather than relative to an abstract model theory alone. Uncertain quantifiers, inheritance, and related PLN constructs are ultimately justified by what an agent has experienced and can re-experience, even when a possible-worlds reading is used as a convenient intermediate formalization.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel insists that uncertain logic for AGI must be *agent-relative and embodiment-grounded*, not merely a calculus of degrees of belief over freestanding sentences. That rules out pure possible-worlds semantics that never touch sensorimotor history, and also rules out treating neural embeddings as a complete substitute for explicit truth-valued relations. The distinctive move is bidirectional: experiential semantics grounds PLN in life history, while possible-worlds formulations can be *derived from* that experiential base—unifying parameters (quantifiers, intensional inheritance, indefinite probabilities) that would otherwise look like unrelated knobs in a probabilistic expert system.
