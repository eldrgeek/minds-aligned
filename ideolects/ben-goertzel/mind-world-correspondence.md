Mind-world correspondence is Goertzel’s principle that efficient general intelligence is heavily constrained by the environment and goal-set it must serve: for a mind to work intelligently toward given goals in a given world, there should be a “nice” mapping from goal-directed sequences of world-states into sequences of mind-states—where composition and structure in the world line up with composition and structure in the mind’s internal dynamics. The principle is offered as a step toward a general theory of general intelligence that starts from adaptation to classes of worlds and goals rather than from a vacuum of pure mathematical generality.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel uses the principle to *constrain architecture*, not merely to restate that agents interact with environments. It rules out both environment-free AGI formalisms that ignore embodiment and world structure, and anthropomorphic “copy the human brain only” programs that never specify which world regularities a mind must track. It departs from standard agent-environment diagrams in AI by claiming that the internal combinatorial structure of a competent mind must systematically mirror goal-relevant structure in its world—linking patternist philosophy and cognitive synergy to design choices about memory types, hierarchical organization, and what kinds of learning processes are worth building.
