Procedure learning is Goertzel's umbrella for how an AGI acquires executable cognitive content—predicates and schemata that can be run—rather than only static facts or associations. In CogPrime, procedures are learned by multiple methods (including program evolution, hillclimbing, and inference-driven construction), with the core framing that learning new skills is substantially a matter of learning programs that operate over the Atomspace and the agent's embodiment.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel distinguishes local extrapolation of procedures from a small set of existing concepts or routines from open-ended program learning that searches a large space of candidate executables. Procedure learning is not mere imitation or policy fitting in isolation; it is one pillar of cognitive synergy that must cooperate with declarative inference, attention, and concept formation. That rules out treating “skills” as only opaque neural policies or only hand-coded operators, and it departs from standard machine-learning “procedure” talk by insisting that general intelligence needs learnable, inspectable, recombineable program-like structures that other cognitive processes can guide, reuse, and revise.
