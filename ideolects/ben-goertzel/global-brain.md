The global brain, in Goertzel's usage, is the emerging planetary-scale cognitive system formed when human minds, software agents, sensors, and open collaborative infrastructures become tightly enough coupled that they function as a distributed intelligence greater than any single participant. It is not a metaphor for the Internet alone, but a real dynamical entity: mutualist exchange networks, automated commons, and open-source AGI projects are treated as substrate through which collective perception, memory, and problem-solving can self-organize at civilizational scale.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel uses global brain as both a scientific hypothesis about ongoing socio-technical evolution and a design target for AGI deployment—favoring decentralized, open, abundance-oriented architectures over closed corporate or military monopolies on superintelligence. This rules out purely individualist AGI narratives in which a single boxed agent is the only locus of mind, and it departs from casual “hive mind” talk by tying collective intelligence to concrete mechanisms (open collaboration, exchange networks, automated commons) and to the same patternist / integrative AGI toolkit he applies to single minds. The global brain is thus continuous with OpenCog/Hyperon work rather than a separate sociological slogan.
