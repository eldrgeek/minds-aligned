Psynet is Goertzel's early complexity-theoretic model of mind: the mind as an autopoietic system of pattern/processes whose dynamics form a hierarchy of attractors, culminating in a dual-network structure that superposes hierarchical and associative organization. Learning is cast as genetic programming over process trees, while persistent beliefs and symbolic content arise as stable systems of mutually creating processes rather than as static stored propositions.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel uses Psynet as a pre-OpenCog bridge between complexity science and AGI design: intelligence is not a single algorithm or a pure neural field, but a self-organizing web of pattern-processes that must stabilize hierarchical abstraction *and* associative linking at once. The model rules out both rigid symbolic architectures without evolutionary dynamics and unstructured connectionism without hierarchical attractors. What is distinctive is that dual-network attractor structure and process-level genetic programming are treated as the natural mathematical skeleton of mind, later feeding patternist philosophy and integrative AGI architectures rather than remaining a standalone psychological metaphor.
