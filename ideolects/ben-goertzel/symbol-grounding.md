Symbol grounding, in Goertzel's usage, is the set of cognitive dynamics by which abstract symbols become connected to concrete physical phenomena accessed through perception and action. Rather than treating grounding as a purely philosophical puzzle, Goertzel's work operationalizes it inside integrative AGI systems (e.g., Novamente acting in a 3D simulated environment) so that linguistic and logical structures acquire sensorimotor meaning through experience, teaching, and interactive learning.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel approaches symbol grounding pragmatically and architecturally: grounding is something an embodied, multi-process AGI must achieve by chaining morphisms and experiential links between high-level relations (prepositions, predicates, concepts) and low-level sensorimotor patterns, not by fiat definition of symbols. That rules out both purely ungrounded symbolic AI and the claim that large statistical language models alone automatically solve grounding without a mind–world loop. It departs from the classic Harnad-style problem statement by embedding grounding inside a full integrative AGI design—shared knowledge graph, learning, and embodiment—where “grounded” means usable for action and further cognition, not only correlationally associated with pixels or tokens.
