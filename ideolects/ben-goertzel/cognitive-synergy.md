Cognitive synergy is Goertzel's name for a dynamical principle of general intelligence: an intelligent system must maintain multiple specialized knowledge-creation mechanisms—typically aligned with different memory types such as declarative, procedural, sensory/episodic, attentional, and intentional—and those mechanisms must actively help one another overcome internal processing bottlenecks rather than operate as isolated modules. In this usage, synergy is not mere modular coexistence or late fusion of outputs; it is ongoing, bottleneck-driven mutual assistance among cognitive processes that share control of the same mind.

<strong>Ben Goertzel's lens:</strong> Goertzel treats cognitive synergy as a near-universal feasibility condition for advanced general intelligence under realistic resource constraints, not as a soft metaphor for "integration." The principle rules out pure single-paradigm AGI designs (logic-only, neural-only, evolutionary-only) as insufficient at human-level scope, and it drives OpenCog/CogPrime architecture choices in which processes such as probabilistic logic, procedure learning, and attention allocation are expected to rescue each other mid-inference. This departs from standard cognitive-science talk of modularity or hybrid systems by making the *cross-rescue dynamics* themselves the design target and by formalizing them (including with category-theoretic tools) as a requirement for feasible AGI rather than an implementation convenience.
