Predictive processing is the cognitive-neuroscientific thesis that hierarchical neural populations continually form expectations about sensory (or lower-level) activity and revise local models when those expectations fail. In computational terms, neurons at one level predict what neighboring or subordinate neurons will do and adjust parameters according to the mismatch between prediction and observation, yielding a unified account of perception, inference, and learning.

<strong>Alexander Ororbia's lens:</strong> Ororbia treats predictive processing as the theoretical warrant for building artificial generative systems, not merely as a story about biological brains. In the Nature Communications neural-coding framework with Kifer, PP is the inspiration that becomes neural generative coding: artificial units predict neighbors, update from local discrepancies, and thereby learn samplers and density models competitive with variational autoencoders—without backprop as the learning law. He often pairs “predictive processing” with hyperdimensional memory in CogNGen-style architectures. The term is broader than his algorithmic label “predictive coding”: PP names the hierarchical guess-and-check doctrine; PC/NGC name the circuits he actually trains.
