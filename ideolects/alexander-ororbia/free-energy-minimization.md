Free energy minimization is the variational principle by which a system continuously reduces a bound on surprise—roughly, the mismatch between expected and observed sensory states—by updating beliefs, policies, or synaptic parameters. In neural implementations, local prediction errors and Hebbian-style plasticity serve as the mechanics that drive the system toward lower free-energy configurations during perception, action, and learning.

<strong>Alexander Ororbia's lens:</strong> Ororbia does not treat free energy minimization as abstract Bayesian branding; he uses it as an operational co-principle with Hebbian learning to instantiate cognitive architectures (including a neuro-mimetic Common Model of Cognition) in substrate that can learn and plan without backprop. With Kelly, free-energy dynamics supply the continuous inference/learning pressure while Hebbian rules provide local plasticity, jointly replacing the global loss-and-gradient stack of generative AI. The term therefore rules out free energy as a decorative synonym for “optimize a loss”: under Ororbia it names the self-evidencing drive that links predictive processing, active inference, and mortal, biomimetic agents.
