Meta-representational predictive coding is a self-supervised predictive-coding formulation in which the system learns by predicting higher-level representations (meta-representations) rather than by reconstructing raw high-dimensional sensory input or by supervised mapping alone. Credit assignment stays within mechanistic predictive coding—local, iterative mismatch correction without backpropagation and without a forward-locked global pass—while the prediction target is shifted from pixels to structured latent codes that support biomimetic self-supervised adaptation.

<strong>Alexander Ororbia's lens:</strong> With Friston and Rao, Ororbia positions this term against both mainstream self-supervised deep learning (contrastive or otherwise) that still depends on backprop, and against classical unsupervised predictive coding that burns capacity reconstructing raw pixels. The distinctive move is *what* is predicted: representations of representations, so that self-supervised structure emerges under neurobiologically plausible credit assignment. The term therefore rules out equating “self-supervised PC” with generative AI–style pixel modeling; under Ororbia it names a biomimetic middle path between pure generative PC and label-driven PC.
