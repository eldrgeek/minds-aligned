Mortal computation is a framing of intelligence in which computational processes are bound to a fragile, energy-consuming physical substrate that can degrade or die; inference, learning, and selection are cast as circularly causal processes that keep a Markov-blanketed system viable rather than as disembodied optimization on immortal silicon. Under this thesis, neuromorphic and biomimetic systems are not optional efficiency tricks but natural consequences of computation that must continually maintain itself against entropy.

<strong>Alexander Ororbia's lens:</strong> With Friston, Ororbia uses mortal computation as a unifying foundation for neuroscience-inspired AI: free-energy minimization, predictive processing, and unconventional hardware are read as answers to the problem of remaining a living (or life-like) system, not merely of maximizing a static objective. The term rules out treating deep nets as immortal software abstractions that can be copied without cost; it instead privileges chimeric agents, organoids, and neuromorphic machines whose credit assignment and architecture must respect substrate limits. In his vocabulary, mortality is the design constraint that makes biomimetic intelligence coherent rather than metaphorical.
