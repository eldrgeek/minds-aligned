CogNGen (the COGnitive Neural GENerative system) is a cognitive architecture that combines a predictive-processing substrate—neural generative coding—with hyperdimensional or vector-symbolic models of human memory. It is designed to sit between high-level symbolic cognitive architectures and low-level spiking neuromorphic simulators, supporting continual learning across diverse tasks while retaining capacities such as long-term memory, single-trial learning, transfer, and planning.

<strong>Alexander Ororbia's lens:</strong> Ororbia (with Kelly) uses CogNGen as the named systems-level home for his neuro-mimetic building blocks: generative coding supplies perception and world modeling; holographic or hypervector memory supplies durable, content-addressable storage. The term is not a generic “brain-inspired agent,” but a specific claim that the Common Model of Cognition can be realized with local Hebbian and free-energy-style dynamics rather than with backprop-trained deep nets bolted onto a production-rule shell. In maze-learning and related work, CogNGen is the architecture that must match or beat deep reinforcement learning while staying neurobiologically plausible and scalable beyond classical cognitive-architecture demos.
