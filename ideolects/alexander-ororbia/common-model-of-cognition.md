The Common Model of Cognition (CMC) is a community consensus sketch of the major functional components shared by leading cognitive architectures—working memory, declarative and procedural long-term memory, perception, action, and control—intended as a mid-level map of human-like cognitive organization rather than as a single proprietary implementation. Realizations of the CMC specify how those modules interact, learn, and support flexible, multi-task behavior.

<strong>Alexander Ororbia's lens:</strong> Ororbia treats the CMC not as a symbolic checklist to re-implement with ACT-R-style production rules, but as a target structure to realize with neuro-mimetic machinery: Hebbian plasticity and free-energy minimization supply the learning and inference laws that wire perception, memory, and control without backprop. With Kelly, a neuro-mimetic CMC becomes a research program for Cognitive Science under generative AI—asking how minds acquire and instantiate those functional roles in biological or artificial substrate. The term rules out equating “cognitive architecture” with either pure high-level symbol systems or opaque end-to-end deep nets; under Ororbia it is the functional scaffold that predictive processing, hyperdimensional memory, and local learning must jointly implement.
