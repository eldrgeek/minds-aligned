Bayesian mechanics is Friston's name for the formal description of how the internal states of a system that possesses a Markov blanket come to look as if they represent—and perform inference about—external states. Paired with quantum, statistical, and classical mechanics in a scale-spanning account of "things," it supplies an information geometry and free-energy principle under which autonomous systems can be read as Bayesian agents maintaining nonequilibrium steady state.

<strong>Karl Friston's lens:</strong> Unlike the informal slogan that "the brain is Bayesian," Bayesian mechanics is a physics of any blanket-bounded thing: particles, cells, organisms, ensembles. Inference is not an optional cognitive add-on but what internal dynamics amount to once conditional independence across the blanket is granted. That rules out treating free-energy minimization as a mere neural coding hypothesis and reframes life-like autonomy as a consequence of statistical separability plus self-organization to nonequilibrium steady state. Bayesian mechanics is therefore how the free energy principle claims compatibility with, rather than competition against, established physical mechanics.
