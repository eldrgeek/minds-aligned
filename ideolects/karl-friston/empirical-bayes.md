Empirical Bayes, in Friston's usage, is hierarchical Bayesian inference under generative models in which the prior expectations that constrain inference at one level are themselves inferred from sensory data by the level above. Rather than fixing priors by hand, higher levels accumulate evidence about the regularities that should serve as priors for lower levels, so that inference and learning implement the same free-energy principle across a cortical hierarchy.

<strong>Karl Friston's lens:</strong> Friston makes Empirical Bayes the organisational principle of hierarchical predictive coding: ascending prediction errors update beliefs about causes; descending predictions supply empirical priors that are context-sensitive and plastic. That rules out flat, single-level Bayesian brains with static priors, and it rules out treating "top-down" influences as mere attention or memory bolted onto bottom-up processing. Priors are generated by the system that uses them. In neuroimaging the same idea reappears as hierarchical (parametric empirical Bayes) models over connectivity parameters across subjects; in process theory it explains how the brain can construct, online, the prior beliefs that make perception and action Bayes-optimal under changing contexts.
