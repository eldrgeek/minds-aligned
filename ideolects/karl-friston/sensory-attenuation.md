Sensory attenuation is the Bayes-optimal reduction in the precision (inverse variance) ascribed to proprioceptive and somatosensory evidence during self-generated movement. In active inference, descending predictions drive classical reflex arcs by creating proprioceptive prediction errors; for those predictions to win over the evidence that one is not yet moving, the precision of that sensory evidence must be transiently withdrawn—equivalently, attention is directed away from the consequences of one's own acts.

<strong>Karl Friston's lens:</strong> Friston does not treat attenuation as a mere corollary-discharge "cancel" of reafference. It is a precision-control solution to a structural conflict: high sensory precision favors detecting externally caused events but blocks action; low sensory precision enables movement but can render self-generated sensations less perceptually available—and can produce characteristic illusions when the balance is wrong. That rules out motor-command accounts in which action and perception are separate pipelines, and it places agency, ticklishness, and certain functional movement phenomena under one free-energy story about when prediction may override sensory evidence.
