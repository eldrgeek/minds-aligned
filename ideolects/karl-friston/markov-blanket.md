A Markov blanket is a set of states that renders the internal states of a system conditionally independent of external states: once the blanket states are known, insides and outsides share no further statistical dependence. In Friston's partition the blanket comprises sensory states (influenced by the outside, influencing the inside) and active states (influenced by the inside, influencing the outside), so that internal dynamics only "see" the world through this statistical interface.

<strong>Karl Friston's lens:</strong> Friston elevates the Markov blanket from a technical device in Bayesian networks to the definition of what counts as a thing—an agent, an organelle, an organism—under the free energy principle. Life and adaptive autonomy are cast as consequences of random dynamical systems that possess (and preserve) such blankets: internal states will look as if they minimize free energy and engage in active inference with respect to external causes. That rules out purely open thermodynamic descriptions without conditional independence structure, and it reframes boundaries as statistical and functional rather than merely anatomical. Nested blankets then explain hierarchical autonomy—cells within organs within organisms—without positing a separate theory of levels.
