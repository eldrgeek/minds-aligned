Interoceptive inference is the application of hierarchical predictive processing to bodily signals: internal physiological states are regulated by autonomic (and related) reflexes that are enslaved by descending predictions from deep generative models of the internal and external milieu. Feeling, selfhood, and emotion are cast as the inferred consequences of those models rather than as raw readouts of visceral sensors.

<strong>Karl Friston's lens:</strong> With co-authors such as Seth, Friston folds interoception into active inference so that homeostasis and allostasis become predictive control—not feedback error correction alone. Autonomic action fulfills interoceptive predictions much as skeletomotor action fulfills proprioceptive ones, with precision (neuromodulatory gain) governing when bodily prediction errors drive belief updating versus physiological adjustment. That rules out purely bottom-up accounts of emotion as labeled arousal, and it ties clinical phenomena of self and affect to failures of generative models and precision weighting over the internal body, continuous with the same free-energy architecture used for exteroception and action.
