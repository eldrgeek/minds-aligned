Affordance, in this usage, is the property of a cue that engenders action because it is predicted to have consequences the agent can fulfil—an invitation to enact a policy under hierarchical inference. Cues with affordance are not mere stimuli to be classified; they are the external or internal signs under which descending predictions about proprioceptive and other outcomes become actionable. How strongly those cues drive behaviour depends on their precision or salience, which in the active-inference account is regulated by dopamine-like neuromodulation balancing bottom-up sensory evidence against top-down prior beliefs.

<strong>Karl Friston's lens:</strong> Friston reframes affordance away from ecological psychology’s purely environment-side “action possibilities” and away from reinforcement-learning “action values.” Affordances are cues that enter hierarchical Bayesian inference as precision-weighted evidence for control; dopamine does not encode reward prediction error as a teaching signal for value, but the precision (confidence) of beliefs that make those cues behaviourally efficacious. That rules out casting dopamine as a scalar reward surrogate and motor control as open-loop commands, and it ties Gibsonian-sounding language to free-energy message passing: to perceive an affordance is already to predict the sensory consequences of acting, with tonic dopamine setting how confidently those predictions enslave behaviour.
