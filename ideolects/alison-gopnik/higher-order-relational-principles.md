Higher-order relational principles are abstract causal rules defined over relations among objects—such as "same causes the effect" or "different causes the effect"—rather than over the intrinsic features of any single object. Learners who grasp such principles can, after a few observations, choose or construct a novel pair that stands in the right relation and thereby produce a new causal outcome they have never seen with those particular items.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> With Walker, Gopnik shows that even toddlers can move past object-bound causal powers to relational structure: the cause is not "this red block" but the higher-order pattern relating blocks. That rules out pictures of early causal learning as limited to feature–outcome associations or only first-order object properties. It also tightens the link between causal learning and overhypotheses: children are learning not only which things work but what kinds of abstract relations are the right level of description—an early form of the theory-like abstraction that rational constructivism attributes to the child as scientist.
