Intuitive theories are the abstract systems of concepts and causal laws that children use to make sense of a domain of experience—minds, objects, living things, or social structure—before and outside formal science. They supply ontology, predictions, and explanations; they go beyond the surface statistics of any single episode; and they can be revised when new evidence fails to fit.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> For Gopnik, intuitive theories are not vague “folk beliefs” or thin associative schemas. They are the psychological counterparts of scientific theories: structured enough to support intervention, counterfactual reasoning, and hierarchical learning of both specific causal hypotheses and more abstract framework principles. What this rules out is treating early knowledge as either fixed modular competence or mere pattern matching. In her reconstructed theory-theory program, probabilistic causal models and Bayesian learning mechanisms are how children acquire, hold, and update these intuitive theories across development.
