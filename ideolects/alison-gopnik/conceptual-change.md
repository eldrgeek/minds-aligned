Conceptual change is the process by which a learner replaces or restructures a prior system of concepts—not merely adding facts inside a fixed frame, but altering the abstract relations, categories, and causal laws that organize a domain. In developmental settings it names how children's intuitive theories shift when evidence and inference demand a new structure.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> Gopnik insists that conceptual change and the possession of abstract structure are not two separate stages or mechanisms. Against views that reserve abstract structure for innate core cognition and assign genuine change only to later language-driven bootstrapping, she argues that probabilistic models and early learning mechanisms already support both structure and revision from the start. What this rules out is a radical discontinuity between “having concepts” and “changing concepts.” In her framework, conceptual change is continuous with ordinary Bayesian theory revision: the same computational principles that induce abstract causal structure also allow that structure to be overturned.
