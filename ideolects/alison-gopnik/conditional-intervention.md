A conditional intervention is an intervention performed while holding another variable fixed (or systematically varying one factor given the state of another), so that the pattern of interventions and outcomes reveals which causal graph is correct—chain, common cause, interactive structure, and so on. The conditional intervention principle is a formal link, central to experimental design and to causal Bayes nets, between what you do (and hold constant) and which structures remain consistent with the data.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> Gopnik and collaborators treat preschool sensitivity to conditional interventions as evidence that children already implement something like the formal assumptions of causal graphical models, not merely associative tracking of co-occurrence or reliance on spatiotemporal cues and familiar mechanisms. That rules out reducing early causal success to contiguity learning or domain scripts alone. It also pins down what "intervention" means in her framework: not any action, but structured experimental contrast that discriminates among candidate causal maps—exactly the kind of evidence a child scientist would need to revise theory-like structure.
