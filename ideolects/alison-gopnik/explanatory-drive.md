The explanatory drive is the intrinsic motivation to seek, produce, and experience explanations of how the world works. In Gopnik's account it is not a cold add-on to inference: the satisfaction of getting an explanation—why something happened, what caused what—functions as a built-in reward that pushes children (and adults) to form and revise theories about causal structure.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> Gopnik's distinctive move is to treat explanation's phenomenology as evolutionarily and developmentally functional: the "aha" of understanding is to theory formation roughly what orgasm is to reproduction—a local pleasure that serves a larger epistemic job. That rules out views on which children only learn when externally reinforced or when solving immediate practical goals. The drive licenses idle curiosity, why-questions, and theory change for their own sake, and it ties the theory theory to a concrete motivational mechanism rather than to disembodied rationality alone.
