Rational constructivism is the view that children actively construct knowledge through rational, often probabilistic inference: they bring structured inductive biases and representational resources to experience, and they revise those structures in principled ways when evidence warrants change. Construction is real, but it is constrained and guided by rational learning procedures rather than unconstrained invention.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> Gopnik coins and deploys rational constructivism to dissolve the classic rationalism–empiricism standoff in developmental psychology. Against pure rationalism, knowledge is learned and revisable; against pure empiricism, learning is theory-laden, model-based, and computationally sophisticated—including unconscious probabilistic inference that need not be slow or effortful. What it rules out is equating "constructivism" with free-form Piagetian stages alone or with associationist accumulation. In her framework it names the philosophical stance that unifies theory theory, causal maps, and Bayesian developmental models as one research program.
