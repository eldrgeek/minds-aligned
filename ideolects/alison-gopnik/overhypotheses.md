Overhypotheses are higher-order hypotheses about the kinds of regularities that hold in a domain—abstract constraints on which first-order causal structures are likely, rather than beliefs about any single mechanism. In causal learning, a causal overhypothesis might encode, for example, whether causes act independently or interact, which features of objects tend to be causally relevant, or how reward and structure are organized across related tasks.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> For Gopnik and collaborators, overhypotheses explain how children generalize beyond the specific blicket, lever, or machine they just saw: exploration is not only gathering local contingencies but testing and refining abstract causal priors that transfer. That rules out accounts limited to one-shot association between particular cues and outcomes. It also connects developmental causal learning to hierarchical Bayesian and RL models, and it sharpens the child-as-scientist claim: scientists, like children, learn not only theories but theories about what good theories look like in a domain.
