The blicket detector is a laboratory machine—typically one that lights up and plays music when certain objects, labeled "blickets," are placed on it—used to give children a novel causal power they can discover only from evidence in the session. Across tasks, children must categorize objects, induce which ones have the hidden causal power, and use patterns of which objects activate the machine to draw causal inferences.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> For Gopnik the blicket detector is not a cute prop; it is the methodological signature of her causal-learning program. By inventing a wholly new causal kind ("blicket") with no prior semantic baggage, the paradigm isolates pure causal structure learning from language, folk physics, or trained associations. It rules out explaining children's success as retrieval of familiar category knowledge, and it departs from looking-time-only infant methods by demanding categorization, induction, and often active choice—showing that preschoolers treat causal power as the basis of kind membership, much as a scientist treats a latent mechanism as the basis of a natural kind.
