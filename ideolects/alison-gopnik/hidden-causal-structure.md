Hidden causal structure is the network of causes, effects, and links that are not directly observed—common causes, latent variables, or unobserved pathways—but that a learner can still recover from the pattern of co-occurrences and from the outcomes of interventions. The structure is “hidden” relative to the surface events; inference makes it available for prediction and action.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> Gopnik treats recovery of hidden structure as a signature of causal-map and Bayes-net learning, not of mere association. Children and adults use dependence, independence, and intervention data to posit unobserved nodes and edges, then use those graphs to guide further reasoning. What this rules out is reducing causal learning to tracking only visible contiguities or trained stimulus–response links. In her framework, the ability to go beyond the observed variables is exactly why children can build theory-like causal models: the interesting structure is often the structure you cannot see.
