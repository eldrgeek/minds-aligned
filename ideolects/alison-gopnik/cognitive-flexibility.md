Cognitive flexibility, in this research program, is the capacity to entertain a broad space of hypotheses and to shift among alternative causal models when the data allow more than one interpretation. It is measured not as generic multitasking skill but as openness in search: how widely a learner samples possibilities before settling on an exploited solution.

<strong>Alison Gopnik's lens:</strong> Gopnik ties cognitive flexibility to human life history. Extended childhood is an evolved window in which flexible, high-exploration hypothesis search is adaptive; with age, search narrows toward efficient exploitation and cultural imitation. What this rules out is treating flexibility as a simple executive-function deficit when children are “distractible,” or as a pure gain that only increases toward adulthood. In her framework, greater childhood flexibility is a feature of the explore–exploit tradeoff: young learners often outperform adults at detecting unusual or higher-order causal structure precisely because they do not lock onto the first locally good model.
