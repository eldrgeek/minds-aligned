Object re-identification is the task of deciding that a currently perceived token is the same persisting individual as one encountered in an earlier episode, including across gaps in observation. In Fields's usage it is a hard computational problem—not a built-in “principle of persistence”—that organisms and artificial systems must solve with heuristics because exact re-identification under Leibniz-style identity criteria is intractable in open environments.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields relocates re-identification from developmental psychology's explanatory primitives to the center of cognitive architecture and, later, of quantum-informed accounts of observation. He argues that what looks like a frame problem in AI is, for humans, largely the object re-identification problem: the information available to decide what did not change after an action is the same information used to track individuals. This rules out treating object permanence as an innate law that explains behavior, and it departs from standard object-file accounts that leave causal constraints on individuation underspecified. Re-identification becomes the observer-side twin of system identification: both are approximate, resource-limited, and foundational to any scale at which an agent carves a world of things.
