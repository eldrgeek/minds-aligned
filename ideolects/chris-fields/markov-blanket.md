A Markov blanket is the statistical and physical boundary that partitions a system from its surroundings so that internal states and external states influence each other only through a mediating set of blanket states. In Fields's usage it is not a mere modeling convenience of graphical models but a general interaction surface: any finite physical system that can be treated as an autonomous unit must present such a surface to everything else, because only then is conditional independence between interior and exterior well-defined.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields and collaborators treat Markov blankets as coextensive with holographic screens and classical information channels between quantum subsystems. The blanket is induced by the interaction itself rather than drawn by an outside theorist; it encodes which eigenvalues of the interaction Hamiltonian are available as classical records. This rules out treating boundaries as fixed ontic walls independent of observation, and it departs from the standard Pearl-style reading in which blankets are local conditional-independence structures in Bayesian networks. For Fields they are scale-free physical facts about separable systems, from molecules and cells through brains and beyond, and they underwrite free-energy and active-inference accounts of life and cognition.
