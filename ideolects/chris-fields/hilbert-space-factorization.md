Hilbert-space factorization is the decomposition of a joint Hilbert space into tensor factors attributed to “system,” “apparatus,” “environment,” or other named subsystems. In Fields's usage this cut is not free metaphysics: once quantum reference frames are required to identify which factor is which, factorization becomes a limited information-processing resource that dissipates free energy—on the order of kT per bit of reduced uncertainty about the provenance of a classically recorded outcome.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Standard decoherence treatments assume outcomes arise from interactions with a pre-factored environment and neglect the thermodynamic cost of establishing and maintaining those factors. Fields shows that factorization is dissipative and observer-relative: you pay to know which subsystem an outcome came from. This rules out treating tensor-product structure as a fixed, costless background of the theory, and it departs from textbook practice in which H = H_S ⊗ H_E is written down without accounting for the classical identification work that makes the product meaningful. Factorization, for Fields, is part of measurement economics, not an a priori gift of geometry.
