A holographic screen is an information-encoding boundary between physical subsystems on which interaction eigenvalues are written as classical records. In Fields's work, classical communication across a boundary and holographic encoding are equivalent: the screen is the place where the interaction between finite quantum systems becomes available as finite-resolution classical information to either side.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields reformulates the holographic principle independently of spacetime geometry, treating screens as induced by separable interactions rather than as surfaces in a pre-existing manifold. Holographic screens function as Markov blankets and classical information channels; quantum reference frames then break the symmetry of the encoding in an observer-relative way, yielding decoherence, contextuality, and classical memory as relations among frames. This rules out treating holography as a special feature of black-hole or quantum-gravity settings alone, and it departs from textbook holographic dualities that presuppose bulk/boundary geometry. For Fields the screen is a general quantum-information fact about any system–environment cut.
