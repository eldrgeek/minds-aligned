A pointer state is a robust, effectively classical state of an apparatus or system component that can serve as a stable record of a measurement outcome. In standard decoherence theory, pointer states are the preferred basis selected by system–environment interaction; in Fields's usage they are the outcomes an observer can only read after first spending finite, finite-resolution resources to identify the system on which those pointer measurements are made.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields insists that pointer-state measurements are never free of a prior identification problem: classical thermodynamics restricts observers to limited observations with which systems must be picked out before pointers can be trusted. When that cost is counted, system identification is at best approximate, and violations of Leggett–Garg and Bell/CHSH inequalities appear as requirements for successful identification rather than as mere experimental curiosities. This rules out treating pointer states as self-interpreting classical dials attached to pre-given systems, and it departs from semi-classical decoherence narratives that take the system–apparatus cut as given. Pointer components and reference components together participate in entanglement-swap accounts of decoherence once the boundary information is made explicit.
