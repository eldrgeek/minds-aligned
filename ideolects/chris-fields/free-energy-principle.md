The free energy principle (FEP) states that any system that persists as a distinguishable thing over time must occupy states that minimize a variational free-energy bound on surprise (prediction error) relative to a generative model of the causes of its sensory exchanges. In Fields's formulation this is not restricted to brains or organisms: it is a generic constraint on how any bounded physical system maintains an interior against an exterior through a Markov blanket.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields extends the FEP beyond classical Bayesian mechanics into a formulation for generic quantum systems, so that classical free-energy dynamics appear as a limit of a more general quantum account. The principle induces structure—compartmentalization, neuromorphic development, hierarchical control—rather than merely describing inference already present in a pre-given architecture. This rules out treating the FEP as only a high-level story about animal brains, and it departs from readings that keep free energy purely thermodynamic or purely psychological. For Fields it is an information-theoretic and physical principle linking measurement, boundaries, and the persistence of living and cognitive systems across scales.
