LOCC (local operations and classical communication) names protocols in which agents act only on their local quantum subsystems and exchange only classical messages. In Fields's framework it is the generic description of multi-agent observation and communication: each party prepares or measures locally, shares classical records, and thereby coordinates identification of systems, outcomes, and shared redundancy without assuming a joint global classical laboratory.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields and collaborators reconstruct LOCC in the language of topological quantum field theory, showing that such protocols generically induce quantum error-correcting codes on agent–environment boundaries and convert entanglement into classical redundancy (as in quantum Darwinism and Bell/EPR scenarios). This rules out treating classical communication as an extra classical channel floating free of quantum dynamics, and it departs from the standard quantum-information reading of LOCC as merely a resource class for entanglement manipulation. For Fields, LOCC is how finite observers implement classical agreement inside a quantum world, and in the operational LOCC setting even spacetime topology becomes observer-relative—an ingredient of the operational recovery of ER = EPR.
