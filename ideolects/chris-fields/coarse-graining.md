Coarse-graining is the process of mapping fine-grained inputs onto fewer, lower-resolution degrees of freedom so that only some distinctions are retained as informative while others are treated as thermodynamic or residual. In Fields's usage it is the generic operation by which finite systems build hierarchical models: each level enacts a coarse-graining of what it receives and, dually, a fine-graining of what it emits when acting or reporting.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields links perceptual coarse-graining to free-energy, neuromorphic morphology, and minimal physicalism: any finite system with limited free energy and morphological degrees of freedom is driven toward hierarchies that coarse-grain inputs level by level. This rules out treating cognition as requiring a special neural substrate that represents the world at full resolution, and it departs from purely statistical coarse-graining in physics as a modeler's convenience. For Fields, coarse-graining is what observers do when they identify objects, reidentify systems across time, and construct scale-free architectures of consciousness and control from molecular pathways up through brains.
