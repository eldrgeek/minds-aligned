A topological quantum field theory (TQFT) is a functorial assignment of Hilbert spaces to boundaries and linear maps to cobordisms, used in physics as a topological, metric-independent model of quantum amplitudes. In Fields's usage TQFT is a general, minimal-assumption language for quantum-state preparation and measurement: sequential observational outcomes appear as finite bit strings induced by measurement operators associated with a holographic screen, represented via functors from diagrams of classifiers to finite cobordisms of Hilbert spaces.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields deploys TQFT not as a specialized tool of pure mathematics or of topological phases of matter alone, but as the formal backbone linking quantum reference frames, holographic screens, LOCC protocols, and quantum error-correcting codes. Cone–cocone diagrams over Barwise–Seligman classifiers supply finite QRFs; the TQFT functor turns sequential measurements into cobordism dynamics and supports topological quantum neural networks. This rules out requiring a fixed background spacetime or a global classical lab before measurement is well-defined, and it departs from textbook quantum mechanics in which topology is optional packaging. For Fields, TQFT is how multi-agent communication and the emergence of boundary spacetimes can be stated with the fewest physical assumptions.
