The holographic principle, in Fields's usage, is the claim that the classical information exchanged in a physical interaction is finite and is encoded on the boundary that separates the interacting subsystems. Interaction is information exchange with strict conservation across that boundary: what one side can write as classical records is exactly what the other side can receive, and those records live on a holographic screen rather than in an unbounded bulk description.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields treats the holographic principle as a straightforward consequence of the quantum information theory of separable systems, not as an isolated postulate of black-hole physics or AdS/CFT. It underwrites a fully general theory of measurement in which finite-resolution classical outcomes appear on screens, get meaning from quantum reference frames, and, under sequential measurement, induce topological quantum field theories. This rules out reading holography as geometry-first dualities that presuppose bulk spacetime; instead Markov blankets, free-energy bounds, and active-inference interfaces are presented as equivalent statements of the same principle across physics, biology, and cognitive science.
