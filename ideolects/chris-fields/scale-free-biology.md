Scale-free biology is the view that living organization—and with it cognition and adaptive problem-solving—is not confined to a privileged level such as the organism or the nervous system, but recurs from molecular and cellular collectives through tissues, organisms, and lineages. When the history of life is read as one continuous cell lineage in reciprocal interaction with its environment, evolution and development become the same multi-scale process seen at different grain sizes.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields and Levin treat variation and selection as bidirectional information flows between scales, and treat “cooperation” and “competition” as scale-relative rather than absolute. Communication, inference, and information processing displace simple one-way causation as the useful language for multi-scale biology. This rules out neural chauvinism—automatically denying cognition or consciousness to basal, nonneural, or synthetic systems—and it departs from evolutionary narratives that wall off development from evolution or that locate agency only in whole organisms. Coupled with minimal physicalism and free-energy thinking, scale-free biology makes Markov blankets, hierarchical Bayesian inference, and active inference available at every scale of life.
