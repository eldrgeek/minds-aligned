Control flow is the ordered, context-sensitive activation of an agent's available perception and action resources—what gets attended, measured, planned, or executed when free-energy resources are limited and the environment is complex. In Fields and collaborators' classical and quantum formulations of active inference, control flow is the architectural problem of deploying Bayesian prediction-error minimization so that the right policies and sensors come online for the right situations rather than running as a single undifferentiated update loop.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Living systems cannot afford full, simultaneous modeling of everything; survival requires a control system that gates perception and action by context. Fields et al. cast this gating in both classical active-inference and quantum-information terms, so control flow sits at the junction of FEP dynamics, Markov blankets, and limited classical bandwidth on holographic screens. This rules out pictures of cognition as unbounded, always-on Bayesian updating, and it departs from software metaphors of control flow as mere sequential program counter movement. Here control flow is thermodynamic and scale-free: cells, tissues, brains, and multi-agent systems all need it to allocate scarce free energy across nested inference problems.
