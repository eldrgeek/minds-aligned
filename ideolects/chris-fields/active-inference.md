Active inference is the process by which a system under the free energy principle minimizes prediction error not only by updating beliefs (perception) but also by acting to change sensory inputs toward those it expects. It is Bayesian prediction-error minimization carried out in a closed perception–action loop, under limited free-energy resources and environmental complexity.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields formulates active inference in both classical and quantum settings, with the classical account as the limit of the quantum one, and treats the control problem—how to deploy limited perception and action resources in a context-specific way—as central rather than peripheral. Control flow in active inference systems can be represented as tensor networks, linking cognitive control to quantum-information architecture. This rules out viewing active inference as merely a psychological or motor-control heuristic for brains alone, and it departs from passive Bayesian brain stories that update models without enacting the world. For Fields it is a generic control regime of living and multi-scale systems bounded by Markov blankets.
