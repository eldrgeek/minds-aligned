Self-representation is the idea that a system can contain an internal model of itself sufficient for self-observation and self-control. In Fields's usage it is a target of impossibility results: for generic physical systems, adding meta-level observational or control capacity cannot yield a complete representation of the whole system from inside, so any self-model is at best heuristic and partial.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields, Glazebrook, and Levin prove that self-observation, self-representation, and self-control are severely restricted once physical interaction is modeled generically—the meta-level component cannot encode the total system of which it is a proper part. This underwrites scale-free accounts of cognition without requiring a privileged neural “self module,” and it rules out complete introspective transparency as a design goal for organisms or robots. It departs from cognitive-science and robotics readings in which an internal self-model is assumed to be arbitrarily refinable, and from naive physicalism that treats self-description as just more data. For Fields, the same boundary logic that yields Markov blankets and holographic screens also yields principled incompleteness of the self: identity and control remain open, approximate, and multi-scale.
