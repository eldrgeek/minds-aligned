Minimal physicalism is the stance that cognition and consciousness require no substrate assumptions beyond those of quantum information theory, and therefore apply from molecular and cellular scales upward—including non-neural and synthetic systems. Consciousness and cognition are characterized in scale-free informational and thermodynamic terms (integrated information, state broadcasting, hierarchical Bayesian inference, coarse-graining, attention switching) rather than as privileges of brains.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields, Glazebrook, and Levin use minimal physicalism to reject the default that a neural substrate is definitional of mind. Basal systems are not merely metaphors for human experience; they are experimentally advantageous instances of the same scale-free architecture, and human autonoetic self-representation is continuous with functions as ancient as bacterial stress response. This rules out neural chauvinism and sharp cognitive/non-cognitive species cuts, and it departs from physicalisms that reduce mind to particular wetware or that smuggle classical computational assumptions in as metaphysics. The work the term does is methodological as much as ontological: if the substrate is only quantum information plus thermodynamics, then scale-free biology and active-inference models become the right joint language for life and mind.
