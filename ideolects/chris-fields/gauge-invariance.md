Gauge invariance is the requirement that physical predictions not depend on arbitrary local choices of description—phases, frames, or other redundant labels of the same underlying degrees of freedom. In the Fields-collaborative setting of the generalized holographic principle, gauge invariance is not an independent postulate pasted onto a field theory: it is entailed by the Hamiltonian description of information flow within a quantum system that maintains a separable joint state, and, when realized in Lorentzian spacetime, yields Poincaré gauge structure and an account of emergent gravity.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Gauge freedom is read as the flip side of holographic encoding on interaction boundaries between separable subsystems. What looks like redundancy of local description is the informational indifference of bulk degrees of freedom once only boundary eigenvalues are exchanged. This rules out treating gauge invariance as a mere mathematical luxury of continuum field theory, and it departs from standard particle-physics pedagogy in which gauges are chosen for convenience after the Lagrangian is written. For Fields and coauthors, gauge structure and gravity-like dynamics emerge from finite information flow across holographic screens, tying high-energy symmetry language to the same observer–system architecture used for cognition and measurement.
