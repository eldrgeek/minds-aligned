A quantum reference frame (QRF) is a physical standard—itself a quantum system or hierarchy of systems—used to identify other systems, fix their pointer bases, and give measurement outcomes semantic content. Without a shared or deployed frame, “which system,” “which observable,” and “what the outcome means” remain undefined; the frame is part of the measurement setup, not an external classical backdrop.

<strong>Chris Fields's lens:</strong> Fields treats QRFs as actively deployed by observers (including apparatuses and biological sensors) on holographic screens. Deploying a frame induces decoherence by breaking the symmetry of the holographic encoding in an observer-relative way; entanglement, contextuality, and classical memory become logical and temporal relations between QRFs rather than free-floating ontic properties. Sharing entanglement as a resource requires a priori shared frames. This rules out absolute, frame-independent system identification, and it departs from semi-classical decoherence models that assume ontic system–environment boundaries in advance. In later work, even neurons are cast as hierarchies of QRFs, tying quantum information theory directly to cognitive architecture.
