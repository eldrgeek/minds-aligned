Compositionality is the principle that the meaning or behavior of a whole is determined by the meanings of its parts together with the ways those parts are interconnected. In Spivak’s applied category theory, that principle is made mathematical: categories, monoidal products, operads, profunctors, and toposes supply the laws under which databases, circuits, dynamical systems, and designs may be assembled without losing control of the interfaces.

<strong>David Spivak's lens:</strong> For Spivak, compositionality is not a vague slogan of modular design but the working commitment that every domain of interest be equipped with an explicit algebra of interfaces—so that “putting things together” is a morphism, not an engineering afterthought. This rules out analyses that only characterize closed systems in isolation or that treat interconnection as informal glue. The distinctive departure from pure category theory’s internal concern with composition of arrows is the external, application-first use of composition: seven sketches pairing real-world systems (databases, resource theories, signal flow, sheaves of behavior) with the categorical structures that make those systems compose.
