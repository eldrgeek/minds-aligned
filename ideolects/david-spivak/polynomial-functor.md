A polynomial functor (on the category of sets) is an endofunctor built from coproducts and products in the shape of a polynomial formula—paradigmatically \(P(X)=\sum_{i\in I}X^{E_i}\)—so that each summand packages a position with a set of directions or ports. The category of such functors and natural transformations supplies a rich setting in which interfaces, interaction protocols, and dynamical systems can be defined and composed with a uniform algebraic language.

<strong>David Spivak's lens:</strong> Spivak treats polynomial functors not merely as a classical construction from functor calculus but as the ambient “abundant” category for mode-dependent dynamics, learning, and interaction: wiring, nesting, and data transformation become operations and structures on \(\mathbf{Poly}\). This rules out modeling open systems only as closed trajectories or only as pure syntax without positions and directions; the distinctive move is to read each polynomial as an interface-with-payload and every morphism as a coherent way of interacting, departing from standard uses of polynomials as mere generating functions or as abstract endofunctors without the dynamical and operadic applications Spivak develops.
