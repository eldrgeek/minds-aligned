Poly (written \(\mathbf{Poly}\)) is the category whose objects are polynomial endofunctors on Set and whose morphisms are natural transformations between them. Spivak presents it as an “abundant” ambient category: it carries multiple monoidal structures, houses interfaces with positions and directions, and supports algebras for mode-dependent dynamical systems that reconfigure their wiring based on collective state.

<strong>David Spivak's lens:</strong> Spivak’s use of Poly is not just naming the category of polynomial functors; it is elevating that category to the default workspace for interaction and open dynamics—where mode dependence, nesting, and data exchange are native operations rather than bolted-on encodings. This rules out frameworks that force reconfigurable systems into a single fixed monoidal category of closed trajectories or pure syntax. The departure from the classical reading of polynomial functors (as combinatorial endofunctors or generating-function devices) is to treat Poly itself as the rich setting in which mode-dependent networks, learners, and interaction protocols live and compose.
