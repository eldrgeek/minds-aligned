A generative effect is a consequence of composing or adjoining systems that cannot be read off from the systems considered separately: the whole produces ordered structure—possibilities, constraints, or resources—strictly beyond the independent sum of the parts. In the Seven Sketches development, such effects are formalized with preorders and Galois connections (adjunctions between ordered sets), so that “joining” parts and “projecting” observations form a monotone adjoint pair whose failure of invertibility quantifies what the interaction generates.

<strong>David Spivak's lens:</strong> Spivak (with Fong) elevates generative effects from a vague systems-theory slogan to the opening pedagogical and technical theme of applied category theory: compositionality is interesting precisely where it is not trivial. This rules out both pure reductionism (the whole is only the parts) and pure holism without calculation (the whole is ineffable). The departure from standard talk of “emergence” is the demand for Galois-theoretic bookkeeping—order-preserving maps and their adjoints—so that what composition generates is measurable, compositional, and comparable across domains from partitions and connectivity to resources and design.
