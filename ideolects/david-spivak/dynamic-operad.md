A dynamic operad is an operadic structure in which the ways components may be wired—and how those wirings compose—can themselves depend on the evolving state of the organization, rather than remaining a fixed syntactic skeleton. Formally developed in the monoidal double category of dynamic organizations (Org), it packages hierarchical assembly so that deep learning architectures, prediction markets, and other adaptive multi-agent systems can change their interconnection law as they run.

<strong>David Spivak's lens:</strong> Spivak uses dynamic operads to push past static operads of wiring diagrams: not only do open systems nest, the *grammar of nesting* can adapt under internal and external pressure. This rules out modeling adaptation solely as parameter updates inside a frozen architecture; reconfiguration of interfaces and protocols is first-class. The move departs from classical operad theory, where the operad is a fixed composition recipe, by making the recipe itself a dynamical, organizational object—compositionality for systems that reorganize.
