Optimal smoothing is a statistical account of motion perception in which the visual system estimates an event’s trajectory using evidence both before and after the moment being reported—formally a smoother rather than a pure filter or extrapolator. Applied to the flash-lag effect, the model fits psychometric curves from experiments with random reversals of motion direction by committing to a location only after sampling a short stretch of the immediate future, thereby improving positional accuracy at the cost of a brief processing lag.

<strong>David Eagleman's lens:</strong> Eagleman and colleagues use optimal smoothing as the computational spine of postdiction: it formalizes why awareness is neither pure motion extrapolation (predicting ahead to cancel delay) nor a latency-difference race between flash and motion. The distinctive move is to treat “using future data” as rational inference under neural delay, not as a paradox—so flash-lag becomes expected under a system that waits for enough of the trajectory before locking a percept, and motion-extrapolation and pure online latency accounts are ruled out by the same formal frame.
