Sensory expansion is the use of a non-invasive channel—typically patterned haptic vibration on the skin—to widen the information available to an intact sense rather than to replace a lost one. Examples include delivering cues about electromagnetic radiation outside the visible spectrum, or boosting high-frequency acoustic detail that residual hearing no longer resolves, so that the existing modality is augmented with dimensions it does not normally carry.

<strong>David Eagleman's lens:</strong> Eagleman and Perrotta place sensory expansion in a triad with sensory substitution (replacing a lost sense such as hearing or vision) and sensory addition (installing a wholly novel sense such as magnetoreception). Expansion is not a softer synonym for substitution: the target sense remains online, and the haptic stream is meant to enlarge its reach rather than stand in for it. That taxonomy treats the adult brain’s capacity to incorporate new structured input as a general plasticity resource, not only a rehabilitation tool for deficit, and it frames wearable haptics as a route to deliberately engineered extensions of human sensory bandwidth.
