Colored sequence synesthesia is the form of synesthesia in which members of overlearned ordinal sequences—especially graphemes (letters and numbers), but also weekdays and months—reliably evoke concurrent color experiences. Eagleman’s laboratory treats it as a network phenotype: grapheme- and color-related regions form a functional “synesthetic” circuit whose connectivity patterns shift when inducers are present, rather than as a simple one-way wire from letter areas to color areas.

<strong>David Eagleman's lens:</strong> Eagleman prefers this category over a flat list of “types of synesthesia” because it unifies the inducers that most often trigger anomalous associations—learned ordinal categories—under one functional claim. The work rules out treating every inducer–concurrent pair as an isolated oddity: colored sequence synesthesia becomes a probe of how slight changes in network properties among ordinary perceptual regions can produce stable extra associations, and it anchors genetic, prevalence, and imaging studies to a single, high-prevalence subtype rather than to the rarest cross-modal pairings.
