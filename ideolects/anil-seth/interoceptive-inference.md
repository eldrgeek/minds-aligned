Interoceptive inference is Seth's extension of predictive coding to the body from within: subjective feeling states (emotions) arise from actively inferred generative models of the hidden causes of interoceptive afferents. Rather than treating visceral signals as raw inputs that are then cognitively appraised, the brain is taken to predict and explain those signals, minimizing interoceptive prediction error through both perceptual updating and autonomic (active) control.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> The distinctive move is to treat interoception with the same hierarchical Bayesian machinery usually reserved for vision and audition, then use that framework to re-explain emotion, body ownership, and conscious selfhood. This generalizes classical appraisal theories (emotion as evaluation of physiological change) into a fully generative, active-inference account and ties feeling to the anterior insula and related circuitry as sites of interoceptive prediction. It rules out a pure bottom-up reading of Jamesian bodily emotion and a purely cognitive, body-optional theory of affect. Where standard predictive processing stays focused on exteroceptive scene construction, Seth's interoceptive inference makes physiological regulation and embodied self the primary target of prediction.
