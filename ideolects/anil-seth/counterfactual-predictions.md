Counterfactual predictions are the brain’s encoded expectations about how sensory signals would change if the agent acted in ways it is not currently acting—virtual sensorimotor forecasts such as how a coffee cup’s visual profile would transform under a grasp, a tilt, or a change of viewpoint. In Seth’s account they supply the predictive-processing implementation of sensorimotor contingencies: mastery of those contingencies is not a free-floating skill but the possession of hierarchical generative models rich in such counterfactuals, and it is that richness that makes ordinary objects feel present as things in a world rather than as mere images or associations.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth deploys counterfactual predictions to fuse enactivist sensorimotor theory with Bayesian predictive processing (his PPSMC package), so that presence is explained mechanistically rather than left as a phenomenological primitive. The notion rules out pure feedforward feature-binding accounts of object perception and pure “here-and-now” coding that never represents non-actual sensorimotor trajectories. It also does diagnostic work: synesthetic concurrents, on his reading, can be vivid without being present because the relevant counterfactual structure is thin or absent—so counterfactual richness, not sensory intensity alone, is what underwrites realness.
