Predictive processing is the principle that brains engage in continuous predictive inference about the causes of sensory inputs by minimizing prediction errors—or informational free energy—across hierarchical generative models. On this view a single formal motif is meant to cover not only exteroceptive perception but also action and the structuring role of body and environment in sensorimotor engagement.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth's distinctive development is a *cybernetic* Bayesian brain: predictive processing is traced less to Helmholtzian passive perception-as-inference alone and more to twentieth-century cybernetic ideas of homeostasis and predictive control. That reframing licenses interoceptive inference (emotion as control-oriented modelling of internal physiological states) and predictive perception of sensorimotor contingencies (including counterfactual structure for presence). It rules out a pure feedforward, stimulus-driven account of perception and a purely epistemic reading of prediction that ignores physiological regulation. Relative to many standard predictive-coding expositions that spotlight external-world accuracy, Seth stresses embodied regulation, active inference, and the continuity of perception, action, and feeling under one free-energy-minimizing architecture.
