Neural signal diversity is a family of quantitative measures—most prominently Lempel–Ziv complexity and related entropy-style indices—applied to spontaneous or evoked neural time series to capture how varied and incompressible the activity is across time and, sometimes, across sensors. In work associated with Seth, high diversity tracks vivid wakeful awareness, lower diversity tracks propofol anaesthesia and deep sleep, and, strikingly, psychedelic states can show diversity even higher than ordinary rest, complicating any simple “more diversity equals more consciousness” slogan while still treating diversity as a robust empirical handle on conscious level.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth treats neural signal diversity as an operational bridge between theoretical claims about dynamical complexity (integration with differentiation) and measurable brain data, without equating the measure with a full theory of consciousness. It rules out relying solely on spectral power, average connectivity strength, or report-based scales when ranking global states. Relative to pure integrated-information theory, diversity measures are practical, data-friendly proxies rather than Φ itself; relative to clinical “depth of anaesthesia” monitors, they are motivated by a principled complexity story and are used comparatively across anaesthesia, sleep, and psychedelics to map the landscape of conscious level.
