The Bayesian brain is the thesis that neural systems approximate Bayesian inference on the hidden causes of sensory inputs: prior expectations are combined with likelihoods from data so that perception, action, and learning amount to continuous minimization of prediction error (or free energy). Under this view the brain does not store a passive copy of the world; it maintains hierarchical generative models whose hypotheses are updated—or fulfilled by action—so as to remain statistically adequate to the sensory stream.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth’s distinctive phrasing is the “cybernetic Bayesian brain”: Bayesian inference is not only about inferring an external scene but about closed-loop regulation of the organism, including interoceptive inference and predictive modelling of sensorimotor contingencies. That formulation rules out treating the Bayesian brain as a disembodied statistician that only explains visual illusions, and it rules out purely feedforward feature-detection accounts of cortical function. Relative to generic Bayesian-brain rhetoric, Seth insists on enactive and embodied integration—control, allostasis, and selfhood—so that Bayesian machinery is cybernetic (goal-directed regulation) as much as epistemic (world modelling).
