Conscious level is the global degree to which a system is conscious—the difference between vivid alert wakefulness and states such as dreamless sleep, coma, or general anaesthesia—as opposed to which particular objects, scenes, or thoughts are currently in experience (conscious content). Operationally it is what quantitative neural measures should track when they aim to be high in waking rest and low in unconsciousness.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth treats conscious level as a dynamical-complexity problem: candidate measures (causal density, integrated information, and later signal-diversity indices) should capture simultaneous differentiation and integration of neural dynamics, because conscious scenes themselves are rich yet unified. This framing rules out equating level with raw arousal, metabolic rate, or any single-channel “on/off” marker, and it separates level science from content science (e.g., binocular rivalry or masking). Relative to purely philosophical level/content taxonomies, Seth’s use is measurement-first and comparative across anaesthesia, sleep, and psychedelic states: level can in principle dissociate from everyday assumptions (as when psychedelic states show increased signal diversity), forcing the concept to stay tied to formal dynamics rather than folk alertness alone.
