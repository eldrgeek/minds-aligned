A beast machine, in Seth's vocabulary, is the organism as a living physiological control system whose conscious selfhood is grounded in continuous, control-oriented regulation of the body from within. The phrase reclaims Julien Offray de La Mettrie's Enlightenment image of the human as a machine, but redirects it: experiences of embodied selfhood are said to arise *because of*, not in spite of, our nature as biological systems that must maintain physiological integrity through allostatic and homeostatic control.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth does not use "beast machine" as a metaphor for crude mechanism or for computational mind-as-software. It names a specific theoretical claim linking interoceptive inference to the phenomenology of self: selfhood is somatic, non-object-like, and subjectively stable over time because generative models are tuned to keep the body viable, not to build a complete picture of an external world. The term rules out Cartesian dualism (self as something that rides on top of the machine) and pure functionalism that severs consciousness from life. It also departs from standard predictive-processing readings that emphasize accurate external-world inference; for Seth the beast-machine story privileges control, allostasis, and the felt body as the origin of conscious selfhood.
