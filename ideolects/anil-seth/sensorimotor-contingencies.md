Sensorimotor contingencies are the structured dependencies between an organism’s possible actions and the sensory consequences those actions would produce—how visual input would change if you moved your eyes, how touch would change if you grasped an object, and so on. In the sensorimotor tradition that Seth engages, ordinary perception depends on mastery of such contingencies: objects feel present in the world because the perceiver implicitly knows how sensory signals would transform under exploration.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth does not leave sensorimotor contingencies as a purely enactive slogan. He reimplements them inside predictive processing as counterfactual predictions within hierarchical generative models—explicitly predicted “what would happen if I acted”—so that mastery becomes probabilistic inference rather than only practical know-how. That move supplies a mechanism classical sensorimotor theory lacked and explains why some experiences (notably many synesthetic concurrents) can be vivid yet lack perceptual presence: they are not embedded in a sufficiently rich web of action-linked contingencies. The term in his usage therefore rules out treating presence as mere sensory intensity, and it departs from pure sensorimotor contingency theory by insisting that contingencies are encoded and exploited as predictive model structure, not only enacted online.
