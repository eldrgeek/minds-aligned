Interoceptive awareness, in the three-dimensional model advanced with Garfinkel and colleagues, is the metacognitive dimension of interoception: how well a person knows the quality of their own interoceptive performance (for example, the correspondence between confidence and actual accuracy on a heartbeat-detection task). It is operationally distinct from interoceptive accuracy (objective behavioural performance) and from interoceptive sensibility (self-reported disposition to notice bodily signals via questionnaires or interview).

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth's contribution is not to treat "awareness of the body" as a single folk capacity, but to insist that interoception fractures into dissociable measurement dimensions with different cognitive, emotional, and clinical correlates. Interoceptive awareness specifically is metacognition about interoceptive accuracy, not a synonym for feeling the body or for accuracy itself. That dissociation rules out collapsing heartbeat-counting scores, questionnaire scores, and confidence–accuracy coupling into one trait labelled "good interoceptors." Within his broader interoceptive-inference framework, the term supplies the empirical language needed to ask how control-oriented models of bodily causes become available (or fail to become available) for explicit self-report—linking laboratory interoception to emotion, dissociation, and embodied selfhood without equating any one score with "the" sense of the body.
