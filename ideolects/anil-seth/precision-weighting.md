Precision weighting is the Bayesian assignment of confidence (inverse variance) to sensory evidence and to higher-level predictions, determining how strongly prediction errors drive belief updating and policy selection. High precision on a sensory channel amplifies its errors; low precision attenuates them. In hierarchical models, precision is itself inferred and is a natural candidate for neuromodulatory control.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> For Seth, precision is not a technical side-parameter of free-energy maths but a bridge from abstract predictive processing to the emotional brain, interoception, and disrupted selfhood. In active interoceptive inference, uncertainty-or-precision maps onto neuromodulation and onto how agranular predictive hierarchies enslave autonomic reflexes; failures of sensory attenuation or of "predicting precision" are then used (e.g. in depersonalisation accounts he co-authors) to explain loss of phenomenal transparency and altered agency. That rules out fixed, context-free sensory reliability and pure content-only models of perception. Distinctively, precision becomes a lever on phenomenology: how vividly, insistently, or "mine" an interoceptive or exteroceptive signal feels depends on the weights the generative model assigns, not only on which hypotheses are entertained.
