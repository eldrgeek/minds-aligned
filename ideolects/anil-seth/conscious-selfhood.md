Conscious selfhood is the experience of being a continuous, embodied subject—of having a self that feels like “me from the inside”—rather than merely a catalogue of self-related beliefs or a narrative identity. In Seth’s usage it is tightly linked to interoception, body ownership, and the predictive regulation of physiological state, not only to reflection, memory, or social self-description.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth argues that theories which tie consciousness mainly to voluntary skeletomotor action selection miss the contribution of internal “action”: autonomic regulation and interoceptive inference that stabilize the living body. Selfhood is thus inferred from control-oriented generative models of the body’s internal condition—being a beast machine—rather than constructed only from exteroceptive mirrors or higher-order thoughts about the person. This rules out reducing the self to narrative or purely cognitive self-models, and it rules out treating body ownership as a late add-on to an otherwise disembodied mind. Relative to standard predictive-processing talk of “the self as a prior,” Seth’s distinctive claim is that the deepest layer of conscious selfhood is affective and regulatory: you experience yourself because you must keep yourself alive.
