The beholder’s share is Ernst Gombrich’s art-historical name for the viewer’s indispensable contribution to what a picture means and how it is seen; Seth reclaims it as a bridge between aesthetics and contemporary neuroscience. In his usage it names the same constructive fact that Helmholtz called unconscious inference and that prediction-error minimization formalizes: perceptual experience of world, self, or artwork is completed by top-down expectations—perceptual, cognitive, affective, and sociocultural—rather than given as a raw copy of the stimulus.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth uses the beholder’s share to make predictive perception culturally legible and historically continuous, not as a metaphor loosely pasted onto Bayesian brain theory. It rules out both naive realism (the world simply impresses itself on a passive observer) and a free-for-all subjectivism in which anything goes: the “share” is constrained by sensory prediction error even as it is generative. Relative to pure computational PEM jargon, the phrase keeps the humanistic and artistic stakes in view—perception as a creative act shared between brain and world—while still answering to hierarchical inference rather than to vague talk of “interpretation.”
