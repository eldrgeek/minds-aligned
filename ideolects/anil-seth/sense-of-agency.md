Sense of agency is the experience of authorship over one's actions and their consequences—the felt "I did that." It is a core facet of conscious selfhood and is often probed with intentional binding (the subjective compression of time between a voluntary action and its outcome), though Seth's group treats that link as empirically contestable rather than definitional.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth co-authors work that severs the automatic equation of intentional binding with agency: in matched virtual-reality conditions, binding-like magnitude effects appear with and without intentional action, so they can be explained as multisensory causal binding rather than as a signature of intention. That move is distinctive: it protects "sense of agency" as a genuine experiential construct while ruling out naive operationalism that equates any binding effect with authorship. In related active-inference treatments of depersonalisation, disrupted agency is further cast as altered precision and sensory attenuation—failures to mark self-generated signals as self-generated—rather than as a missing homunculus. Agency, for Seth, is inferred and fragile, not a primitive motor tag.
