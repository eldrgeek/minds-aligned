Phenomenological control is a normally distributed, stable trait capacity to generate experiential changes—hallucinations, analgesia, nonvolition, ownership illusions, and related alterations—so that subjective experience conforms to expectancies arising from imaginative suggestion or perceived experimental demands. The resulting experiences are typically reported as involuntary even though they track top-down goals and demand characteristics.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> In the collaborative program with Lush, Dienes, and colleagues, phenomenological control is not a fringe hypnosis curiosity but a methodological and theoretical lever on consciousness experiments. Substantial links between hypnotisability-class trait scores and classic effects (rubber hand illusion, mirror-sensory synaesthesia) imply that much of what labs treat as pure perceptual or multisensory machinery may be expectancy-driven experiential control. This rules out naive readings of illusion paradigms as transparent windows onto body-ownership or synesthetic circuitry without measuring trait control, and it complicates simple bottom-up stories of those effects. Relative to standard “top-down attention” or bare predictive-processing talk, the concept is more specific: a metacognitive, suggestion-sensitive ability that can fabricate phenomenology while feeling passive—central for anyone who, like Seth, builds theories of presence, self, and controlled prediction from experimental report.
