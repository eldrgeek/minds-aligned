The embodied self, in Seth’s vocabulary, is the experienced subject as it arises from predictive, control-oriented modelling of the body—especially interoceptive signals—rather than as a pure cognitive ego or a mere collection of exteroceptive body parts. Subjective feeling states, body ownership, and the stability of “being someone” are treated as consequences of actively inferred generative models of the causes of bodily afferents, tightly coupled to emotion and physiological regulation.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth’s “embodied self” is not embodiment as a soft reminder that minds have bodies; it is a mechanistic claim that selfhood is built from the inside out through interoceptive inference. That rules out Cartesian inner theatres and purely social or narrative selves that float free of autonomic control, while still allowing higher-order self-models to elaborate the basic somatic core. Relative to standard predictive processing about vision and action, he relocates the self’s origin in control of the internal milieu; relative to classical appraisal theories of emotion, appraisal becomes hierarchical Bayesian inference on interoceptive causes. Psychiatric alterations of ownership and selfhood are then readable as failures or recalibrations of those embodied predictive models.
