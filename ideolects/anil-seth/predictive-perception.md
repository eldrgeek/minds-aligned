Predictive perception is Seth’s name for the view that conscious perceptual experience is not a passive readout of sensory data but an active, generative interpretation: the brain continually forms a “best guess” of the external (and internal) causes of its sensory signals by combining top-down expectations with bottom-up prediction error. In this picture, what we see, hear, and feel is shaped as much by prior knowledge, affect, and sociocultural expectation as by the current sensory barrage—an idea Seth traces from Helmholtz’s unconscious inference through contemporary prediction-error minimization and the Bayesian brain.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth uses predictive perception as the experiential face of predictive processing: not merely a computational slogan, but an account of why perception feels like contact with a world while remaining deeply constructive. The term rules out pure bottom-up “shutter” models of experience and pure cognitive-impenetrability views that deny expectation a role in phenomenology. Relative to generic Bayesian-brain talk, Seth stresses the parallel with art-historical “beholder’s share” and the extension from exteroceptive scenes to self and emotion; relative to bare predictive coding, he ties predictive perception to presence, action, and the organism’s need to stay viable, so that perception is generative without collapsing into unconstrained fantasy.
