The free energy principle (FEP) is a proposed unifying theory of brain function on which self-organizing systems that maintain themselves in non-equilibrium steady states can be described as minimizing an information-theoretic functional—variational free energy—thereby approximating Bayesian inference about the hidden causes of their sensory exchanges with the world. In the brain sciences it is cast as integrating Helmholtzian perception-as-inference, machine learning, and statistical thermodynamics into one account of action, perception, and learning.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth’s lab treatment of the FEP is characteristically both committed and demystifying: free energy is the mathematical spine of predictive processing and active inference, but it is not left as an opaque slogan. The 2017 mathematical review (with Buckley and colleagues) opens the assumption structure of a widely used biological implementation, supplies a complete agent-based model, and insists that significance for neuroscience depends on what those assumptions license. This rules out treating FEP as a free-floating metaphysical guarantee that “brains minimize free energy” without specifying generative models, precision, and embodiment. Relative to pure Fristonian expositions, Seth routes free-energy minimization through cybernetic regulation, interoception, and consciousness science—using it as scaffolding for controlled predictive perception and beast-machine selfhood rather than as an end in itself.
