Active inference is the idea that agents minimize prediction error not only by updating internal generative models (changing beliefs to fit sensory data) but also by acting so that sensory data come to fit existing predictions. In the interoceptive domain this includes autonomic and behavioural control that holds physiological variables within predicted ranges, so that descending predictions enslave reflexes rather than merely representing bodily states after the fact.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth uses active inference to reframe emotion, selfhood, and bodily regulation as control-oriented prediction rather than passive sensing of the body. Interoceptive predictions set the targets of homeostatic and allostatic control; feelings of emotion and the experience of being an embodied self are the inferred consequences of that control loop. This rules out a purely epistemic reading of Bayesian brains (inference only updates beliefs) and a purely feedforward interoceptive account (emotion as labelled afferent flux). Relative to Friston-style formalisms that stress free-energy minimization in general, Seth’s distinctive emphasis is enactivist and somatic: active inference is the mechanism by which a “beast machine” stays viable, and conscious selfhood is grounded in internal action as much as in voluntary skeletomotor choice.
