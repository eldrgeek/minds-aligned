Controlled hallucination is Seth's compact name for ordinary perception under predictive processing: experience is a top-down generative construction—the brain's best guess of the hidden causes of noisy, ambiguous sensory data—kept in register with the world by continuous prediction-error minimization. What we see, hear, and feel is not a passive readout of the senses but an actively inferred scene, constrained by sensory input so that the “hallucination” is useful rather than free-floating.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> For Seth the phrase is not a metaphor for pathology and not a claim that reality is unreal. It does positive theoretical work: it unifies Helmholtzian unconscious inference, the Bayesian brain, and Gombrich's beholder's share under one phenomenological slogan, and it makes perceptual presence, illusions, and clinical hallucinations differences of degree of constraint rather than differences of kind. It rules out naive realism (perception as transparent window) and pure bottom-up feature assembly. Where a standard reading of “hallucination” means percepts without corresponding external objects, Seth's controlled hallucination reclaims the word for successful everyday seeing—controlled by sensory prediction errors, priors, and action—so that clinical hallucinations become cases where that control loosens or mis-specifies the generative model.
