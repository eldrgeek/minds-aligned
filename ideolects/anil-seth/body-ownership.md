Body ownership is the experience that a particular body (or body part) is one's own—the sense of "this is my hand, my body"—as distinct from mere location, agency, or visual recognition of a limb. In Seth's experimental programme it is measured and manipulated most famously via rubber-hand and virtual-hand illusions, where synchronous multisensory cues can transfer ownership toward a fake or virtual limb and asynchronous cues weaken that transfer.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth treats body ownership as a product of hierarchical multisensory (and interoceptive) inference about the causes of bodily signals, not as a fixed anatomical given. The distinctive move in the cardiac rubber-hand work is to show that interoceptive signals—cardio-visual feedback locked to the heartbeat—modulate ownership online, and that individual interoceptive sensitivity predicts illusion strength. That rules out purely visual–tactile accounts of the rubber-hand effect and pure bottom-up body maps. Ownership becomes evidence for conscious selfhood grounded in predictive models that bind exteroceptive and interoceptive domains; when those models are rewritten by timing, presence, or cardiac synchrony, the felt boundary of "my body" moves with them.
