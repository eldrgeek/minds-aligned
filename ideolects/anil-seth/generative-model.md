A generative model, in Seth’s usage, is the hierarchical probabilistic machinery by which the brain predicts the sensory consequences of hidden causes in the external world and in the body’s internal milieu. Perception, action, and autonomic regulation are all cast as inversion or enlistment of that model: descending predictions explain away ascending prediction errors, and when they cannot, either the model updates (perceptual inference) or the organism acts—motorically or viscerally—to make the world or body conform to the prediction (active inference).

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth’s distinctive move is to insist that the generative model is not only about exteroceptive scenes but is deep enough to include interoceptive and proprioceptive causes, so that emotion and embodied selfhood fall under the same scheme as vision. That rules out treating the body as a mere input channel and treating feeling as a separate appraisal module bolted onto “cold” perception. Relative to generic machine-learning talk of generative models, Seth’s version is cybernetic and control-oriented: the model exists to keep the organism within viable physiological bounds, not merely to reconstruct a passive world model for contemplation.
