Interoceptive accuracy is the objective dimension of interoceptive ability: how well a person performs on behavioural tests that track actual internal signals, classically heartbeat detection or discrimination, as distinct from how interoceptive they say they are. In the three-dimensional model developed with Garfinkel and colleagues, it is dissociated from interoceptive sensibility (questionnaire or interview self-evaluation) and from interoceptive awareness in the metacognitive sense (how well confidence tracks true accuracy), so that “being good at feeling the body” is no longer a single undifferentiated trait.

<strong>Anil Seth's lens:</strong> Seth needs this distinction because his wider theory of interoceptive inference and embodied selfhood depends on carefully measured bodily prediction and error, not on folk self-report of “body awareness.” The term rules out collapsing interoception into anxiety questionnaires or into a single heartbeat-counting score treated as a gold standard without metacognitive context. Relative to looser clinical uses of “interoception,” accuracy is the performance anchor that lets individual differences, emotion, and pathology be linked to predictive models of the internal milieu without confusing confidence, belief, and actual sensory tracking.
