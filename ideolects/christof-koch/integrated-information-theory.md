Integrated information theory (IIT) is a formal account of what consciousness is and which physical systems can have it. It begins from phenomenological axioms of experience—intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, and exclusion—and translates them into postulates about the causal structure a physical mechanism must realize to support consciousness.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> For Koch, IIT is the theoretical complement to the NCC program: correlates alone cannot explain why cortex supports experience while cerebellum does not, nor settle edge cases such as infants, non-mammalian animals, damaged brains, or machines. IIT rules out equating consciousness with intelligent behavior, report, or global workspace access alone; a system can be highly intelligent yet have little integrated information, or simple yet highly conscious if its causal structure is irreducible. Koch uses IIT to move from “where the lights are on” empirically to a claim about the physical substrate of consciousness as a maximum of irreducible cause–effect power, not mere complexity or neuron count.
