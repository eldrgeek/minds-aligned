Reentrant activity is recurrent, looping neuronal signaling among cortical (and cortico-thalamic) populations, as opposed to a one-pass feedforward cascade from receptors to higher areas. In reviews of the neural correlates of consciousness, Koch and collaborators treat the contrast between sustained or reentrant dynamics and purely phasic feedforward activity as a central axis along which conscious percepts can be distinguished from unconscious sensory processing.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> For Koch, reentry is not decorative anatomy; it is a candidate dynamical requirement for experience. A fast feedforward sweep can support rapid categorization and visually guided behavior without awareness, whereas the NCC for a specific percept is more often tied to recurrent interactions that stabilize a winning coalition over tens to hundreds of milliseconds. This rules out equating consciousness with any early sensory spike train that merely registers a stimulus, and it aligns with IIT-style insistence that systems whose causal architecture is strictly feedforward—even if complex—cannot support the irreducible, integrated cause–effect structure of a conscious state.
