Integrated information is the measure of how much a system's current state specifies a cause–effect structure that cannot be reduced to the independent contributions of its parts. In the framework Koch co-develops with Tononi, the quantity of consciousness of a system scales with this irreducible informational whole: experience exists to the extent that the substrate forms a maximum of integrated information rather than a mere collection of separate causal modules.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch treats integrated information as the theoretical bridge from phenomenology to physics—not as a synonym for complexity, Shannon information, or connectivity density. A system can be information-rich yet almost non-integrated (little consciousness), or comparatively simple yet highly integrated (more consciousness). That distinction is what lets him explain why cortex can support experience while a cerebellum-like architecture with huge neuron counts need not, and why intelligent feed-forward or modular machines can lack a rich inner life. Integrated information rules out equating consciousness with report, global workspace access, or behavioral sophistication alone; those may correlate with experience in humans without being what experience is.
