A quale is a particular conscious experience—the felt quality of a specific moment of seeing, hearing, pain, or thought—taken as a whole rather than as a report or behavioral capacity. In the integrated-information framework Koch co-develops with Tononi, each quale is the particular form of experience associated with a system’s maximally irreducible conceptual structure.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch does not treat “quale” as a mysterious leftover after science has explained function; under IIT it is what the theory is about from the start. Quantity of experience is graded by integrated information, while quality—the shape of this quale versus that one—is fixed by the system’s cause–effect structure (composition, information, integration, exclusion, intrinsic existence). That rules out reducing a quale to external discrimination, verbal report, or computational role alone: two systems can compute the same mapping while only one realizes the intrinsic structure that is the experience. It also lets Koch say why cortex can support rich experience and cerebellum, for all its neurons, may not—the issue is the form of causal integration, not mere complexity or intelligence.
