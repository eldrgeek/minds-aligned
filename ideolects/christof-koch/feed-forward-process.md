A feed-forward process is a largely unidirectional cascade of activity through hierarchical visual areas—from early feature extraction toward higher selectivity—without requiring recurrent loops or reentrant feedback to complete the computation. In Koch's usage it names a timing and architecture claim: under tight temporal constraints (for example masked natural-scene categorization), selective visual behavior can be launched by this first pass alone.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch deploys feed-forward processing to separate what cortex can achieve in a single sweep from what may require recurrent excitation for full, reportable awareness or detailed object analysis. Showing that categorization-level selection can be triggered feed-forward rules out the assumption that every sophisticated visual act needs top-down loops, while leaving open—and often suggesting—that the richer, sustained machinery of conscious seeing may still depend on recurrent cortico-cortical interactions. The term thus polices timing arguments in the NCC literature: latency alone cannot prove a process is conscious, and early selectivity is not automatically a complete account of visual awareness.
