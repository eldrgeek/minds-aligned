Visual awareness is the conscious experience of seeing—having a particular visual percept as part of one's subjective state—rather than merely detecting, discriminating, or acting on visual information. In Koch's early program with Crick it is the chosen form of consciousness for experimental attack: if the mechanisms of conscious seeing can be isolated, other modalities of experience should become tractable by analogy.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch does not treat visual awareness as a loose synonym for vision or attention. It is the experiential side of the visual system—what it is like for a stimulus to be seen—set against the many unconscious visual computations that still support behavior. That framing licenses a research strategy of holding retinal input fixed while awareness flips (rivalry, flash suppression, masking) and hunting the neuronal difference that tracks the conscious percept. It also rules out equating awareness with early feature extraction alone, or with any process that can run without the subject having a corresponding visual experience.
