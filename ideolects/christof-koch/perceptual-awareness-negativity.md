Perceptual awareness negativity (PAN) is an event-related potential component—typically a negative-going wave over posterior sensors in a roughly mid-latency window—that covaries with whether a sensory stimulus is consciously perceived. It is advanced as a candidate physiological marker of sensory consciousness, timed earlier and localized more posteriorly than later, frontal-heavy signatures often linked to post-perceptual processes.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch uses PAN to sharpen the empirical NCC search against confounds of report, decision, and working-memory updating. Where P3b-like late positivities can track task relevance and access rather than experience, PAN is offered as closer to the onset of the conscious percept itself—content-specific sensory awareness rather than the global broadcast or verbal report that may follow. That move aligns with his broader argument that the minimal machinery for conscious content sits more in posterior sensory systems than in prefrontal executive circuits, and it rules out treating any late, report-correlated ERP as automatically the signature of consciousness.
