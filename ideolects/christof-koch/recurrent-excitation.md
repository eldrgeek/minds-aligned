Recurrent excitation is the dense local excitatory feedback among neighboring cortical neurons that constitutes much of a cell’s synaptic input. In Koch’s circuit-level account, populations in visual cortex use this effective “network conductance” to amplify weak feedforward signals while keeping discharge roughly proportional to stimulus strength, so selectivity (for direction or orientation, for example) can be sharpened without pure serial feedforward wiring alone.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch emphasizes that most cortical synapses are cortico-cortical, so realistic models of cortical function—and later of candidate NCC mechanisms—cannot be restricted to one-pass feedforward cascades. Recurrent excitation supplies a concrete biophysical middle path: amplification and selectivity emerge from feedback loops, yet runaway seizure-like activity is avoided by balanced design. That stance rules out purely serial hierarchical stories of vision and prepares the ground for reentrant and recurrent accounts of sustained, content-bearing activity. It also contrasts with systems (or phases of processing) that remain essentially feedforward and are therefore poor candidates for the full, integrated causal structure Koch associates with conscious experience.
