The physical substrate of consciousness is the concrete physical system—its elements and causal relations—that is identical with a conscious experience, rather than merely reliably co-occurring with reports of experience. It is what a complete theory must identify once correlates have been charted: which mechanism, at which grain, actually constitutes the experience.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch, with Tononi and collaborators, uses “physical substrate” to mark the step beyond the NCC: correlates can be necessary or jointly sufficient in practice yet still leave open what experience is. Under IIT, the substrate is the complex that maximizes irreducible integrated information—the cause–effect structure specified by a system for itself—not the whole brain, not every active area, and not any substrate that merely computes the same function. That rules out functionalist substrate-independence as automatic: two systems can be behaviorally equivalent while only one realizes the right intrinsic causal architecture. It also rules out simply equating consciousness with widespread activity, metabolic load, or intelligence; the substrate is fixed by integrated information, exclusion, and composition, not by outer performance.
