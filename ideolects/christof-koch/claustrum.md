The claustrum is a thin, irregular, sheet-like neuronal structure lying beneath the inner surface of the neocortex near the insula. Anatomically it is striking: it receives projections from almost all cortical regions and sends projections back to almost all of them, making it a candidate hub for rapid, widespread cortico-cortical coordination.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Working with Francis Crick, Koch treats the claustrum not as a generic association area but as a possible anatomical answer to how the brain unifies features into a single conscious percept. The proposal is functional and experimental: the claustrum's reciprocal, nearly cortex-wide connectivity could enable information to travel widely within the structure and help bind modality-specific content into an integrated experience. This usage is deliberately speculative yet empirically framed—it rules out ignoring deep, obscure structures in favor of only primary sensory maps or prefrontal report centers, and it motivates targeted tracing, stimulation, and lesion work rather than purely psychological accounts of binding.
