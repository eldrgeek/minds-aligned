Top-down attention is selective, task- or goal-driven attentional selection—the analyzer that enhances some inputs and filters others—as distinct from bottom-up saliency capture. In Koch's psychophysical program it is the form of attention most carefully pried apart from consciousness: subjects can attend to perceptually invisible objects, and under some conditions can become conscious of an isolated object or the gist of a scene with little or no top-down attention.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch insists that top-down attention and consciousness are not two names for one process. Their joint factorial manipulation can yield opposing effects, which undercuts the default assumption that selective attention is necessary and sufficient for awareness. Functionally he casts attention as analyzer and consciousness as synthesizer: attentional selection can operate without a corresponding experience, and experience can arise without full top-down selection. That dissociation is methodological as much as theoretical—it keeps the search for the NCC from collapsing into a search for the neural correlates of attentional selection alone.
