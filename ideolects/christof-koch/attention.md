Attention is the selective amplification or prioritization of some sensory or cognitive contents over others, supporting enhanced processing, report, and action. In Koch's experimental vocabulary it is a distinct brain process from consciousness: one can attend without conscious seeing, and one can be conscious of something with little or no top-down attention.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch's decisive move is the double dissociation. Against the common habit of treating attention and consciousness as near-synonyms, he argues they are two processes that often travel together but can come apart—top-down attention without awareness, and awareness with minimal attentional load. That split cleans the NCC search: phenomena explained only by attentional selection must not be mistaken for the machinery of experience itself. It also legitimates paradigms (continuous flash suppression, dual-task and near-threshold designs) that hold attention and visibility orthogonal, so the neuronal signature of conscious content is not confounded with the signature of selection.
