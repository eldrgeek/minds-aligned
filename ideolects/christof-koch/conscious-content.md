Conscious content is the specific experiential material that fills awareness at a given moment—the face, motion, color, pain, or thought that one is conscious of—rather than the global condition of being conscious at all. Content-specific neural correlates are the minimal neuronal mechanisms jointly sufficient for a particular percept once a conscious level is already in place.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch's program hinges on separating contents from levels and then asking which cortical territories specify the former. In the front-versus-back debate, clinical and neuroimaging evidence is used to argue that posterior cortex is more tightly bound to specifying conscious contents, while much prefrontal activity tracks report, attention, decision, and task structure. That reading rules out identifying consciousness with any frontal global-workspace process that merely makes a content available for verbal or motor output. Experimentally, it demands paradigms that hold report and task demands fixed while varying what is seen, so that the NCC for a content is not mistaken for the machinery of communicating that content.
