The hard problem of consciousness is the question why and how physical processes in the brain are accompanied by subjective experience at all—why there is “something it is like” rather than only information processing, report, and control. It is distinguished from “easy” problems about functions that consciousness supports, such as discrimination, integration, and verbal report.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch does not treat the hard problem as a conversation-stopper. In his program it becomes empirically approachable once one maps the footprints of consciousness—the NCC—and tests competing theories (including IIT) against shared, preregistered predictions. The 2021 “Making the hard problem of consciousness easier” stance, with Melloni, Mudrik, and Pitts, frames adversarial collaboration and open science as ways to force theories to commit on measurable neural signatures rather than retreat into irresolvable metaphysics. That rules out both pure quietism (experience forever outside science) and pure functionalism that dissolves experience into performance. For Koch the hard problem is made easier by isolating content-specific correlates, dissociating them from attention and report, and asking which physical architectures can actually constitute a quale.
