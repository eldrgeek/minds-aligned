The back of the cortex, in Koch's usage, names the posterior cortical territory—especially sensory and association regions behind the central sulcus—hypothesized to specify the contents of conscious experience. It is defined contrastively against the front of the cortex: the question is whether prefrontal systems are necessary for conscious perception itself, or mainly for report, attention, and cognitive control built on top of experience.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch coauthors treat the front-versus-back dispute as an empirical fork in the NCC program. Clinical and neuroimaging evidence is marshaled to argue that the back of the cortex is more tightly tied to specifying conscious contents, while much frontal activation reflects task demands, decision, and verbal or motor report rather than the percept as such. That stance rules out simple prefrontal theories that identify consciousness with global broadcasting from frontal workspace circuits alone, and it sharpens experimental design: paradigms must dissociate experience from report if they are to locate the true content-specific NCC rather than its cognitive consequences.
