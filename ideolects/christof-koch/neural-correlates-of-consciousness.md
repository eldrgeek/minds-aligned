The neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) are the minimal set of neuronal events and mechanisms jointly sufficient for a specific conscious experience. In Koch's research program the phrase names an empirical target: which patterns of brain activity, in which regions and at which times, track the presence and content of a percept rather than merely enabling perception, report, or attention.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch treats the NCC as a concrete, experiment-driven stepping stone toward the physical substrate of experience, not as a full theory of consciousness. He systematically separates the NCC proper from enabling factors (arousal systems, blood flow, eyes open), from consequences of being conscious (verbal report, motor selection), and from closely related but dissociable processes such as attention. The NCC research strategy—binocular rivalry, masking, continuous flash suppression, sleep and anesthesia comparisons—is meant to isolate the content-specific, minimal sufficient machinery, often pointing away from pure prefrontal report circuitry and toward posterior cortico-thalamic networks, while still remaining compatible with later theory-building in integrated information theory.
