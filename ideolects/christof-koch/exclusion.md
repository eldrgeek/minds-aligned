Exclusion is the phenomenological claim that each experience is definite in content and grain: at any moment there is one set of experiential distinctions actually present, not a fuzzy average or simultaneous stack of alternative experiences. In integrated information theory this axiom becomes a physical postulate: among nested and overlapping candidate mechanisms, only the maximum of irreducible cause–effect power specifies the conscious entity and its quale; smaller or larger systems that overlap it are excluded as additional conscious wholes.

<strong>Christof Koch's lens:</strong> Koch deploys exclusion to block plural, overlapping consciousnesses in the same substrate and to fix the grain of the physical substrate of consciousness. Without exclusion, every subset and superset of neurons could claim its own experience, leaving no principled account of why you have one unified scene rather than a cloud of micro-experiences. Exclusion therefore does work that mere correlates cannot: it selects a unique complex as the consciousness-bearing system and rules out heaps, aggregates, and feed-forward networks as additional or substitute subjects of experience, even when they contain many active parts.
