Eliminative connectionism is the family of neural-network approaches that aim to account for cognition without mechanisms that manipulate symbols—without variables, structured bindings, or rule-like operations over typed representations. In Marcus’s framing, such models try to replace classical symbol systems with distributed pattern association alone, treating symbolic structure as something to be eliminated rather than implemented.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus treats eliminative connectionism as a research program that fails precisely where human generalization succeeds: people can apply universal relations to novel instances (e.g., “if glork then frum”), which he argues requires operations over abstract symbols, not only similarity-weighted associations. The term is not a neutral label for all connectionism; it marks the *eliminative* claim—that symbols are dispensable—and Marcus uses that claim as a foil for his algebraic alternative, in which networks may still implement symbol-manipulating machinery rather than replace it.
