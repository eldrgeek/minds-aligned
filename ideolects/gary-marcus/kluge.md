A kluge is an engineering-style makeshift: an inelegant, cobbled-together solution that somehow works. Applied to cognition, the term names the claim that the human mind (and language in particular) is not a cleanly optimized design but a historical patchwork of ancestral mechanisms, constraints, and later add-ons that evolution retained because they were good enough.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus elevates “kluge” from slang to a theoretical stance about mental architecture: language and mind are products of descent-with-modification under evolutionary contingency, not proofs of optimal design. The term rules out both intelligent-design perfectionism and naive adaptationism that treat every cognitive trait as a finely tuned module, and it departs from standard engineering or classical-AI readings of “architecture” by insisting that haphazard construction is itself evidence—imperfection becomes a diagnostic of evolutionary history rather than a bug to be ignored.
