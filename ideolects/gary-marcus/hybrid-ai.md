Hybrid AI is an approach that combines learned statistical machinery with explicit knowledge, structured representations, and reasoning—rather than relying solely on general-purpose learning from ever-larger datasets and compute. Cognitive models sit at the center: systems that can represent relations, use prior knowledge, and draw inferences beyond pattern completion.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus positions hybrid AI as the constructive alternative to scale-only deep learning: the next decade of progress, on his account, requires knowledge-driven, reasoning-based substrates that yield robustness where pure end-to-end nets fail. The term rules out both classical GOFAI purity (symbols without learning) and deep-learning maximalism (learning without structured knowledge), and it departs from industry “hybrid” marketing by tying the hybrid claim to specific cognitive ingredients—models, common sense, and symbol-manipulating operations—not mere ensembles of neural modules.
