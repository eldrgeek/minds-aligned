Trustworthy AI, in the Marcus–Lenat usage, is artificial intelligence that can be relied on for correctness, predictability, and interpretability—not only for producing outputs that sound or look plausible. It names a target beyond generative fluency: systems whose reasoning can be audited, whose failures are bounded, and whose commitments to world knowledge and logic are explicit enough to support real-world deployment under risk.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus uses trustworthy AI as the normative contrast class to large language models and pure deep-learning pipelines that optimize for plausible continuation. With Lenat, he argues that generative models are systematically less than trustworthy because they lack robust reasoning, world models, and interpretable structure—and that lessons from symbolic knowledge systems (e.g., Cyc) sketch an alternative path. What this rules out is equating benchmark success or uncanny fluency with reliability. The departure from generic "AI safety" talk is architectural: trustworthiness is not only alignment of goals but a demand for hybrid systems with structured representations, commonsense knowledge, and mechanisms that go beyond next-token prediction.
