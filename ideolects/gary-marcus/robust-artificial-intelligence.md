Robust artificial intelligence is Marcus’s program for AI that remains reliable outside narrow training regimes: systems that reason with cognitive models, use structured knowledge, and do not depend only on ever-larger datasets and compute. Robustness here means flexible, trustworthy performance under novelty, incomplete information, and distributional shift—not merely high benchmark scores on familiar tasks.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus defines robust AI as a deliberate alternative to the dominant pure-scale, general-purpose learning agenda. He centers a hybrid, knowledge-driven, reasoning-based approach as the substrate for richer AI than pure deep learning currently allows. That rules out the idea that more data and bigger models alone will yield human-like flexibility, and it departs from both classical GOFAI nostalgia and end-to-end empiricism: symbols, innate structure, and hybrid architectures are engineering requirements for robustness, not philosophical luxuries.
