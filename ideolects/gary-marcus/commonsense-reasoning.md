Commonsense reasoning is the suite of everyday inferences humans draw about the physical and social world from sparse, partial, or noisy information—knowing, for example, how containers hold, spill, and constrain objects without a complete physical model of every situation. In the AI program Marcus advances with collaborators such as Davis, it is a target competence that current statistical systems still systematically fail to secure.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus treats commonsense as the hard core of trustworthy AI, not as soft cultural knowledge that more web-scale data will inevitably absorb. Work on containers and other mundane domains is used to show that people reason productively under *radically incomplete* information, which pure pattern-matching and end-to-end deep learning do not reliably reproduce. The term rules out benchmark-chasing that equates next-token fluency with understanding, and it departs from narrow task AI by making incomplete-world reasoning a design requirement for hybrid systems that combine learning with structured knowledge and inference.
