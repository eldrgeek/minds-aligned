The atoms of neural computation are hypothesized elementary, reusable computational primitives realized in neural circuitry—basic operations from which more complex cognitive functions could be composed. Marcus, Marblestone, and Dean pose the question whether the brain depends on a set of such elementary computations, including mechanisms that could support operations like symbolic variable binding rather than only homogeneous pattern association.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> For Marcus this phrase is a bridge from cognitive-level symbol manipulation to implementational neuroscience. It challenges the assumption that a single generic learning algorithm or uniform cortical microcircuit is enough: if minds need structured representations and variables, the brain needs neural "atoms" capable of realizing those operations. What it rules out is pure eliminative connectionism as a complete story of neural computation—networks that only interpolate statistical regularities without compositional machinery. The distinctive move is not to deny neural networks but to demand a richer parts-list: reusable computational elements that make algebraic, structured thought biologically plausible rather than epiphenomenal.
