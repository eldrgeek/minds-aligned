Structured representations are encodings that preserve constituency, variables, and hierarchical relations so that operations can apply to roles and types rather than only to particular tokens. In the generative tradition Marcus defends, they are the substrate that makes algebraic generalization and compositionality possible in language and thought.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> With Berent, Marcus insists there is “no integration without structured representations”: reconciliation between generative linguistics and connectionism cannot erase the algebraic hypothesis. Most connectionist models remain antithetical to that hypothesis if they lack true structure-sensitive machinery. The term rules out peace treaties that treat unstructured distributed patterns as sufficient for grammar, and it departs from soft “hybridization” talk by treating structured representation as non-negotiable—not an optional implementation detail, but the dividing line between systems that generalize by algebra and those that only approximate by association.
