Descent with modification is Darwin's principle that complex biological traits arise by gradual inheritance and change from earlier forms. Marcus applies it to cognition and language: apparent modular systems and domain-specific capacities are products of evolutionary history in which earlier circuits, constraints, and learning biases are reused, retuned, and cobbled together rather than engineered de novo for each function.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> For Marcus, descent with modification is the evolutionary bridge between nativism and the messy architecture of real minds. It underwrites his modularity story and his "kluge" theme: language and other cognitive systems need not be pure, optimally designed modules to still be partly innate and partly specialized. What it rules out is both blank-slate empiricism (everything learned from scratch in each individual) and idealized modular clean-room design (each faculty a purpose-built, isolated organ). The distinctive use is to reconcile domain-specificity with evolutionary continuity—structure can be innate and specialized while still being historically derivative, imperfect, and shared with earlier systems.
