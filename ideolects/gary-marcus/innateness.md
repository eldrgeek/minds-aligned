Innateness, in Marcus’s usage, is the stock of internal structure—architectures, priors, representational formats, and learning biases—that an organism or system brings to experience rather than extracting wholly from data. It is not a claim that every fact is pre-specified, but that powerful learning depends on substantial built-in machinery that constrains and enables generalization.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus mobilizes innateness against the AI fashion of treating “tabula rasa” scale learning as an ideal. Using AlphaZero-style systems as a test case, he argues that success often showcases *more* engineered inductive bias than boosters admit, not less. In his framework, innateness rules out pure blank-slate connectionism as a full theory of mind or route to robust AI; it departs from popular readings that equate innateness with rigid genetic scripts by emphasizing abstract, reusable computational priors—structure that makes flexible learning possible rather than freezing behavior in place.
