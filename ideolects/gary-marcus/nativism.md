Nativism is the thesis that minds arrive with structured biases and representational predispositions that shape learning, rather than as fully open inductive engines written only by experience. For Marcus, those predispositions are developmental and biological: they constrain how plasticity is deployed so that children acquire language and other cognitive systems with the speed and character that pure empiricism cannot explain.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus’s nativism is explicitly *plasticity-compatible*. The apparent paradox—that brains are highly plastic yet cognition looks richly structured early—is resolved by treating innate structure as the organization of learning mechanisms, not as a frozen inventory that experience cannot touch. The term rules out empiricist and anti-innate critiques that equate nativism with fixed, unmodifiable hardwiring, and it departs from popular “blank slate vs. destiny” framing by making developmental biology the medium in which innate contributions and experience jointly build the mind.
