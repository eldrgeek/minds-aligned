Variables are abstract placeholders in mental representations—open slots that can be bound to different instances so that a single relation (identity, “same as,” algebraic pattern) applies uniformly to novel items, including ones never encountered in training. They underwrite open-ended generalization: the same rule or constraint holds for arbitrary fillers of a role.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus treats variables as indispensable cognitive machinery, not optional notation. Models that try to capture phonotactics or rule learning without variables may fit native-like statistics yet fail when humans generalize identity restrictions to non-native phonemes; equipping the model with variables restores the human pattern. The term rules out pure feature-co-occurrence accounts of algebraic generalization and departs from standard statistical-learning readings by locating the power of “rules” in variable-mediated relations rather than in tabulated surface frequencies alone.
