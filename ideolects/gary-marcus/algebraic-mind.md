The algebraic mind is Marcus’s name for a view of cognition in which the mind systematically manipulates structured representations—binding variables, composing relations, and generalizing operations beyond the particular items on which they were learned. Algebra here is not literal school mathematics but the capacity for open-ended, instance-independent operations over types and slots.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus uses the algebraic mind to reject a forced choice between “the mind is a symbol computer” and “the mind is a neural network.” His distinctive move is integrative: a large parallel network can still *be* an algebraic symbol-manipulator if it implements variable binding and structure-sensitive operations. That rules out pure eliminative connectionism and pure unstructured statistical learning as complete stories of human thought, and it sets the bar for both cognitive theory and AI: models must explain free generalization to novel instances, not only interpolation within training distributions.
