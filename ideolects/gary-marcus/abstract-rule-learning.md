Abstract rule learning is the ability to extract identity-based, algebra-like regularities from sequences—patterns such as ABB, AAB, or ABA—and to generalize those relations to novel items that never appeared in the training set. Marcus and colleagues showed that infants can do this rapidly from brief exposure, treating repeated and nonadjacent positions as abstract roles rather than as fixed sound or shape tokens.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> For Marcus, infant abstract rule learning is empirical pressure for algebraic machinery in early cognition: success on “ga ti ga”–style grammars is not mere transitional-probability tracking or item memorization. The construct rules out pure associative accounts that cannot apply a learned relation to wholly new vocabulary, and it departs from standard “statistical learning” rhetoric by treating rules as first-class abstractions—facilitated especially by speech—rather than as epiphenomena of surface co-occurrence statistics.
