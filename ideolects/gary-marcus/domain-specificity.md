Domain specificity is the claim that aspects of mind—especially linguistic and other cognitive content—can be specialized for particular problem domains rather than arising solely from a single, fully general learning device. In Marcus’s developmental-genetic framing, genes can influence such specialized structure not by encoding whole modules as finished blueprints, but by shaping developmental pathways that bias how domain-relevant representations form.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus defends domain specificity against the objection that “genes only code for proteins,” arguing that genetic influence on specialized cognitive content remains coherent once development is taken seriously. The term rules out blank-slate constructivism and pure domain-general connectionism as complete stories of language and thought, and it departs from crude one-gene–one-module nativism by tying specificity to cascade-like developmental biology rather than to innate, fully specified organs handed down as finished software.
