Statistical learning is the capacity to extract distributional regularities—co-occurrence frequencies, transitional probabilities, and other surface contingencies—from experience. In language acquisition research it is often proposed as a primary engine by which infants segment speech and discover structure without explicit instruction.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus does not deny that learners track statistics; he denies that statistics alone exhaust grammar. Against reconcilist slogans that ask whether grammar “starts where statistics stop,” he holds that complex cognition depends on a *mixture* of statistical mechanisms and algebraic (rule-based, variable-mediated) ones. The term rules out pure associationist reductions of linguistic knowledge to tabulated frequencies, and it departs from both anti-statistical nativism and statistics-only empiricism by treating statistical learning as real, necessary, and still limited.
