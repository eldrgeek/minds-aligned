Symbol manipulation is the capacity to operate over abstract symbols, variables, and structured bindings—applying the same relation to novel instances rather than only reactivating similarity-weighted associations among previously experienced patterns. In Marcus’s cognitive architecture, minds (unlike eliminative networks that forgo such machinery) include, among other resources, mechanisms that treat roles and types algebraically so that universal relations generalize beyond the training distribution.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus uses symbol manipulation as the positive counterpart to eliminative connectionism: the 1986 parallel-distributed-processing bet that the mind might proceed without symbols is, on his reading, still untenable a generation later. The term rules out pure pattern-associator accounts of systematic linguistic and logical generalization, and it departs from both classical GOFAI (which sometimes treated symbols as the whole story) and anti-symbolic connectionism (which treats them as dispensable) by insisting that neural implementations must *realize* symbol-manipulating operations, not eliminate them.
