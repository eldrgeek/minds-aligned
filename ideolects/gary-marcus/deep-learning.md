Deep learning is the family of multi-layer neural network methods that, since roughly 2012, have driven large gains in speech recognition, image recognition, and game playing by learning hierarchical statistical features from large datasets. In Marcus’s usage it names both that technical approach and the broader research program that treats scaling such networks as the main path to intelligence.

<strong>Gary Marcus's lens:</strong> Marcus treats deep learning as a genuine but incomplete advance: useful for perceptual classification and surface pattern match, yet systematically weak on abstraction, compositionality, causal reasoning, and data-efficient generalization. The term, in his critical appraisal, rules out equating deep learning with artificial general intelligence and departs from hype-driven readings by listing concrete architectural limits—rather than celebrating benchmarks alone—and arguing that deep nets must be supplemented by hybrid, knowledge- and reasoning-centered techniques.
