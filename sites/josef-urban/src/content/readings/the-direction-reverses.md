For twenty years the flow of this work runs one way: formal libraries out, into forms that automated provers and learners can consume. In 2026 the newest paper in the archive runs the other way. "130k Lines of Formal Topology in Two Weeks: Simple and Cheap Autoformalization for Everyone? (Short Paper)" (sole author) describes formalizing "a large portion of the general topology from the Munkres textbook (which has in total 241 pages in 7 chapters and 39 sections)," reporting a project running since 21 November 2025 that had produced 160k lines of formalized topology by 4 January 2026, "most of it (about 130k lines)... in two weeks." An earlier paper, "Exploration of neural machine translation in autoformalization of mathematics in Mizar" (2020), is the same idea at experimental scale. The archive reads the 2026 short paper as the point where autoformalization stops being a research demonstration and starts being a throughput claim — with the question mark in its own title left standing.
