The oldest work here and the newest are the same idea in different clothes. In reservoir computing and liquid state machines — "Stability and Topology in Reservoir Computing" (2010) and "Topological constraints and robustness in liquid state machines" (2011, first author, with Larry Manevitz) — computation is a property of a fixed network's dynamics and topology, with only a simple readout trained. Fifteen years later, "Reservoir Computation with Networks of Differentiating Neuron Ring Oscillators" (2025, senior author) is still building reservoirs; and "A Little Rank Goes a Long Way" (2026, first author) says in its own abstract that its construction "is formally analogous to Reservoir Computing unfolded along the depth axis of a feedforward network." A 2010 idea reappearing as a claim about how large language models are trained. Between those poles sit spiking networks and the BindsNET toolchain, bioelectric circuits, and ecosystems. The archive reads this as one question carried across substrates rather than a change of field.
