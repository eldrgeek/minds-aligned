Hazan is one of sixteen authors of "Stop treating `AGI' as the north-star goal of AI research" (2025). He co-signed it; he did not lead it, and the argument belongs to all sixteen. The paper calls itself a position paper and argues that "focusing on the highly contested topic of `artificial general intelligence' undermines our ability to choose effective goals," naming six traps: Illusion of Consensus, Supercharging Bad Science, Presuming Value-Neutrality, Goal Lottery, Generality Debt, and Normalized Exclusion. It asks the field to prioritize specificity, center pluralism, and widen inclusion. The archive notes a consistency worth flagging, as its own inference and not as anything the authors claim: if competence is not located in a system's trained parameters — the thread running from this record's reservoir work to its 2026 frozen-backbone result — then treating scale toward "general intelligence" as the field's organizing target is a questionable premise on technical grounds, not only political ones.
