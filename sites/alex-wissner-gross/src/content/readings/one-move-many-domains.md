Twenty-one records across nanotechnology, cellular automata, surface physics, granular media, finance, telerobotics, cardiology and defense machine learning do not look like a research programme. Read closely they share a move: identify a physical constraint that everyone else has been treating as a free parameter, and compute with it. "Relativistic statistical arbitrage" (2010, with Cameron E. Freer, Physical Review E) observes that "light propagation delays between geographically separated exchanges" are now relevant to high-frequency trading, and calculates where on Earth to stand — the speed of light as a trading-desk siting problem. "Diamond stabilization of ice multilayers at human body temperature" (2007, with Efthimios Kaxiras) reports a "remarkable increase in the melting point of ice" on a modified diamond surface — the physics of a coating, costed. "Pattern Formation without Favored Local Interactions" (2007, sole author) removes the assumption that some local rule is privileged and asks what pattern survives. The archive reads Causal Entropic Forces as the same move aimed at the largest available target: treat intelligent behaviour as something a thermodynamic quantity already constrains, rather than as something only design produces.
