The 2019–2025 arc moves the same commitment outward twice. First from features to entities: "MONet" (2019, last author) argues that "the ability to decompose scenes in terms of abstract building blocks is crucial for general intelligence," and decomposes scenes into objects with no supervision; "SIMONe" (2021) does it over video while jointly inferring viewpoint. Then from entities to embodiment: "Alchemy" (2021) builds a meta-reinforcement-learning benchmark because past ones were "either too simple to be inherently interesting, or too ill-defined"; the SIMA project trains agents to follow free-form language instructions across many commercial 3D worlds, calling that "a key challenge for creating general AI"; "SIMA 2" (2025) is built on a Gemini foundation model and described as an interactive partner rather than a command-follower. The archive reads this as the disentangling programme cashed out — structured representations were always argued for on the grounds that they would make agents data-efficient and transferable, and this is the part of the record where agents are actually built.
