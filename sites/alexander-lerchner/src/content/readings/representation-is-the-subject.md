title: "The archive's reading: the subject was never the substrate"

This record splits visibly in two — computational neuroscience to 2006, DeepMind from 2016 — and the split is misleading. The early papers ask what a balanced cortical population encodes and how stably: "Mean field theory for a balanced hypercolumn model of orientation selectivity in primary visual cortex" (2006, first author, with Gustaf Sterner, John Hertz and Mandana Ahmadi) determines "self-consistently both the firing rates and the firing correlations, without being restricted to specific neuron models." The later papers ask the same question of a latent code: β-VAE (2017, last author) opens by arguing that "learning an interpretable factorised representation of the independent data generative factors of the world without supervision is an important precursor for the development of artificial intelligence that is able to learn and reason in the same way that humans do." The archive reads the gap not as a change of field but as a change of substrate for one question: what should a system carry about the world, and what makes that carrying good? Neurons, then latents, then objects, then agents.
