The pivot in this record is not a change of subject but the application of one idea to a new domain. Communication became reliable when signals were coded into a finite set of symbols: a discrete alphabet lets you detect and correct errors instead of accumulating them, and lets a message have an exact description. "Reversibly Assembled Cellular Composite Materials" (2013, with Kenneth Cheung, Science) applies that to matter — a structure built from a finite set of mass-produced parts with "integrated mechanical interlocking connections", which can therefore be assembled, disassembled and reused, and which the paper reports responds "as an elastic solid with an extremely large measured modulus for an ultralight material." "Discretely assembled mechanical metamaterials" (2020, Science Advances) states the contrast directly: such materials have "historically... been made as continuous, monolithic structures with additive manufacturing, which affords high resolution and throughput, but is inherently limited by process and machine constraints." The archive reads the entire post-2013 programme — voxels, material-robot systems, walking machines, swarms — as consequences of that one substitution: make the material digital, not just the design.
