If a structure is built from a small set of interchangeable parts, then the machine that places those parts can be simple, and can be built from the same parts. That observation drives the archive to an endpoint it names openly. "Hierarchical assembly of a self-replicating spacecraft" (2017, with Will Langford, Amanda Ghassaei and Ben Jenett) proposes a hierarchical model for machine design aimed at "exponential space exploration via self-replicating spacecraft (known as Von Neumann probes)", motivated by the observation that conventional manufacturing "is complicated by the need to integrate thousands of unique parts." "Self-replicating hierarchical modular robotic swarms" (2022, Communications Engineering, last author) reports a discrete modular material-robot system "capable of serial, recursive" construction — robots assembling more robots. "A Discretely Assembled Walking Motor" (2019) and "Discretely assembled walking machines" (2020) are the intermediate steps: mechanisms, not just structures, emerging from the same voxel vocabulary. The archive reads this as von Neumann's universal constructor pursued as an engineering programme rather than a thought experiment.
