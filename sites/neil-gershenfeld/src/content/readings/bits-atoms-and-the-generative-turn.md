For most of this record, the design is authored by a person and the contribution is in how it gets built. The newest papers change who proposes the shape. "Speech to Reality: On-Demand Production using Natural Language, 3D Generative AI, and Discrete Robotic Assembly" (2024) describes a system that "transforms speech into physical objects using 3D generative AI and discrete robotic assembly," making design and manufacturing "more accessible to people without expertise in 3D modeling or robotic programming" — and names the gap it exists to close: "while generative AI models can produce a wide range of 3D meshes, AI-generated meshes are not directly suitable for robotic assembly." "MechStyle" (2025) adds mechanical simulation so a stylized generative output remains structurally sound; "Making Physical Objects with Generative AI and Robotic Assembly" (2025) studies the fabrication constraints directly. The archive reads these as the point where two decades of discrete-assembly work becomes the substrate a generative model needs in order for its output to be a thing rather than a picture of a thing.
